It's a shock! These cancers don't feel at all.

Author: Jijio Date of issue: 11 August 2026 Update: 11 August 2026 Reading time: About 4 minutes

A lot of people think there's gonna be obvious pain or discomfort in cancer. Some of these cancers are born "no pain, no itch" and grow in the middle of the body, and often miss the best treatment time when the symptoms occur. Medically, these early cancers are often described as “silent killers” or “hidden cancer”. Pancreas, ovarian, liver and lung cancer are among the most likely to be neglected and most often detected in the middle and late stages of life, according to clinical experience and many medical institutions. Understanding why they are so hidden and how to improve early detection opportunities through regular inspections may be the key to survival.

The main reasons why these cancers are difficult to detect at an early stage are the depth of the organ, the lack of a painful neurosis, the ability to provide for them, or the extreme ambiguity of the early symptoms, which can be misinterpreted as a general stomach incompetence, fatigue or aging. Many patients were found unexpectedly even during routine health check-ups and other diseases. The following is an in-depth account of the most difficult cancers.

Pancreatic cancer: silent killing of the King of Cancer Hands.

The pancreas are deep in the abdomen, surrounded by stomach and intestine organs, which are almost unreachable. Early pancreatic cancers, with about 70% of all symptoms, or non-specific manifestations such as mildly ill appetites, abdominal abdominals, and thinning, can easily be treated as eating problems or stress. The tumor is growing at a relatively rapid rate, and it may take months from early to later. Most of them are in the middle and late stages when there is a visible yellow flu, continuing back pain, weight loss or new diabetes diagnosis. The overall survival rate is low for five years, but it could be significantly increased if the tumor were to be operated at an hourly time. High-risk groups, including people with family history, long-term smoking, chronic pancreas, obesity or diabetes, should be particularly alert and discussed with doctors about progress video screening.

ovarian cancer: female "silent killer"

The ovaries are deep in the pelvic cavity, the space is wide and the early tumour hours are almost impervious to the surrounding organs and therefore do not cause obvious malfeasance. About 40 to 50 percent of the patients were diagnosed in the third or fourth phase. Early warning signs are often seen of persistent abdominal problems, large abdomen, easy to feed, reduced appetite, low abdominal strangulation, indigestion, frequent urination or experiency, which are often mistaken for stomach problems, menopause or urinary infections. There is currently no recognized and effective universal screening tool, and CA125 has some value for blood and pelvic ultrasound, but has limited sensitivity and specificity. If a woman aged 50 years or over or has a family history of ovarian and breast cancer continues for more than two to three weeks, she must be treated as soon as possible.

Liver cancer: The liver has a powerful ability to make it "no pain."

Even if a significant proportion of his liver tissue is affected by a tumor, as long as the remaining function is normal, the liver function may remain in the normal range, and the patient can eat and feel free of it. Many cases of liver cancer are detected unexpectedly during a medical examination or other abdominal ultrasound, at which time the tumor may have been quite large. High-risk groups include hepatitis B or C carriers, hepatitis cirrhosis, chronic alcohol-drinking and fat-heavy. It is recommended that high-risk persons undergo blood and liver ultrasound tests every six months, which is currently recognized as a more effective combination of early detection.

Lung cancer: lung is suffering from arrhythmia, early almost unconscious

The lung suffers from a lack of apparent pain, early tumors usually do not cause inflammation or pain response, and patients can hardly feel it through their own feelings. Early lung cancer in bed is mostly detected through low-dose chest computer faults (LDCT) or routine health tests; more often than not, it continues to be treated for cough, blood, chest pain, respiratory distress, etc. Even non-smokers may suffer from second-hand smoke, air pollution, professional exposure or family history. If you are over 50 years of age, have a long history of smoking or have high-risk jobs, you should discuss with the doctor the suitability for low-dose breast computer failure.

In addition to the four above-mentioned types, the early years of bowel cancer may also be characterized as a problem of diet, with only a slight change in excrement or occasional abdomen; some of the kidney cancers also suffer from early symptoms due to their location. The common denominator of these cancers is that they are vague or non-existent, so it is very dangerous to "wait for a second look."

How to improve early detection opportunities?

First of all, know your own risks: family history, chronic diseases, life habits can affect the chance of cancer. Second, the five major cancer screenings recommended by the National Health Agency are accepted according to age and risk, and, depending on the situation, the liver ultrasonic, low-dose chest computer faults, and gynaecology ultrasonics are increased. Thirdly, it is important to keep an eye on the slight changes in the body: the unknown causes are lighter, weary, persistent, abdominal, defecating or urinating habits change, skin or eye white turn yellow, etc., and should be treated if not painful. Finally, maintaining a healthy lifestyle, smoking, drinking, maintaining the desired weight, balanced diet and discipline can reduce some of the cancer risks. Remember, early detection is still the most critical factor in improving survival and does not make "no feeling" an excuse to delay treatment.

The most difficult cancers remind us that the "silence" of the body does not mean health. Through correct knowledge and regular testing, we can take the initiative to take the health initiative. If you have any doubts, please immediately consult a professional doctor and take early action if you are to truly safeguard your future and that of your family.
