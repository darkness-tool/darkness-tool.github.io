What the hell is Superman? Another Y chromosome becomes "breed bad"? Medical truth-telling!

Author: Handsome. Date of issue: 1 May 2026 Update: 1 May 2026 Reading time: About 8 minutes

What exactly is the Ultra-Hama competition? Why has it been popular lately, even called the "demon gene"? In our daily lives, we rarely hear the word "super-male," but the recent birth of a pregnant woman found news of chromosomal chromosomal abnormality in her child, prompting the medical name to rush. Many people have associated them with "breed evil" and "criminalization" and have caused much discussion. In fact, the medically called "47, XYY" syndrome of the Ultra-Material Concertation is an anomaly in the number of sexual chromosomes. The normal male chromosomes were 46, including 22 for the common chromosomes and a pair of sex chromosomes, while the male had an additional Y chromosome, which became 47, i.e. nuclear 47, XY. This is not a hereditary disease, most of which is the result of an error in the reduction and division of the sperm formed by the father, and does not make a person a "super-bad person" overnight. Let's take a scientific look at what's going on.

The Ultra-Hama syndrome was first described by scientists Sandberg and others in 1961 and was later referred to as Jacobs or XYY syndrome. It has a rate of about 1:1,000 live boys, or about one in every 1,000 newborn boys. This may sound a lot, but because most patients are not unusual in their appearances and are not suffering from significant physical defects, more than 80 per cent of the cases remain undiagnosed. It can only be discovered when pre-delivery tests, chromosome nuclei analysis or other health problems are performed.

What's the reason for the super-sugar?

The event was mainly related to the fact that the chromosomes in the course of the father's sperm formation were not isolated. Two Y chromosomes were not properly separated at the second post-separation stage of sperm generation, resulting in the creation of an abnormal sperm (YY sperm) containing two Y chromosomes. When this abnormal sperm collide with the normal eggs with X chromosomes, the fertilized eggs form 47, XYY chromosomes. Some 84 per cent of the cases originated from this system, while another 16 per cent may have been misled in the early filament of embryonic cells, resulting in embedded cells of 46, XY and 47, XY. It is worth noting that this anomaly is not inherited by parents, but by random events, and that normal couples may produce such babies.

What are the most common characteristics of the ultra-H perch?

Most of the paralysing patients have a fairly normal appearance, with no apparent unusual appearance or severe deformities. The most common feature is a larger body, usually higher than the average of the same age, especially in childhood, where the height of the adult body is more than 180 cm. The body may be stronger, but the abdominal fat is sometimes higher. A small number of patients may have mild physical characteristics, such as larger teeth, a slightly wider eye range, flat feet or reduced light muscle tension, but these are not all available and are not the decisive basis for diagnosis. With regard to reproduction, most patients have normal gland function and fertility, and a few may have reduced sperm numbers or slightly lower fertility, but can be solved with medical assistance.

At the intellectual level, the IQ of ultra-male patients is usually within the normal range, but may be about 10 to 15 minutes below the average of other family members. Some people may experience difficulties in developing their language, reading and writing, or learning, and need extra educational support. ADD, autism syndrome or other minor behavioural problems may be slightly higher than The general population, however, does not mean that all patients have serious symptoms. Many super-male men, with proper education and family support, are able to live, work, and even perform well in sports or certain areas of power.

Where does the term "criminal gene" or "naturally bad species" come from? What about the medical truth?

A study dating back to the 1960s on the link between male and female recruitment and criminal tendencies. When scientists found a higher percentage of the XYY nuclear type among mentally handicapped or prison men, it was assumed that an additional Y chromosome could lead to an increase in offensive or violent behaviour. The idea was quickly spread, even amplified by the media and video, and created the stereotype that "Suman is a natural criminal". However, the subsequent large-scale epidemiological studies and systematic statements have clearly pointed out that there are serious deviations in the early study samples - – The investigation is itself a high-risk group and does not represent the general super-male population. According to modern medical studies, there is no direct causal link between male and female recruitment and crime. Any crime is the result of a complex combination of social, environmental, educational and personal factors that cannot simply be attributed to an extra chromosome. In fact, the vast majority of male super-males are ordinary law-abiding citizens who live a normal life.

The medical profession no longer advocates the use of the misleading popular term "super-male", but suggests that it be called the 47 XYY syndrome. Experts have stressed that labelling such groups of people as "devil genes" or "bad species" not only lacks scientific evidence but also creates unnecessary social discrimination and psychological pressure. On the contrary, early detection and appropriate educational support, psychological counselling and medical intervention can help them better adapt to society and to their own strengths.

What if the baby is found to be ultra-heo?

The pre-natal inspection found that parents were often nervous and anxious when 47, XYY or embedded. But experts agree that this is not a reason for inducing. Super-sugars are usually expected to grow well, with most children growing up with the right support. Parents are advised to seek the help of a specialist genetics consultant to consult with doctors, to gain a full picture of the case, and to plan early early childhood development support programmes, including language therapy, learning guidance and behavioural management. At the same time, society should reject prejudices and view these children in an inclusive and scientific manner. After all, everyone has a unique genetic combination, and the value should not be defined by a chromosome. Ultra-Masculinism is only part of the diversity of human heritage, and knowledge of the truth can avoid fear and misunderstanding and give every life the respect and love it deserves.
