Why is it that once the bees are over-excited, they are prone to a terrible war? A deadly crisis for beekeepers.

Author: Keung Date issued: 10 September 2026 Update: 10 September 2026 Reading time: About 7 minutes

In a quiet bee-keeping field, a seemingly insignificant disturbance can turn into the end of the whole beehive! Many beginners have often wondered why the normal, cooperative and orderly bee family, with its extraordinary excitement, can easily lead to a terrible act of bee theft. It is indistinguishable from the survival instincts of the bees, the odour recognition, and the brutal resource competition of nature.

To understand this, we need to know first of all what a bee-thrower is. In nature, when outside honey-producing plants are depleted and the honey season ends, some of the workers are at risk of stealing honey from other nearby hives in order to sustain the growth and survival of their communities. This unworked behaviour, if not properly controlled, expands rapidly as soon as it begins.

Why would the excitement become a fuse?

When beekeepers perform inappropriate open-box inspections during the season when honey is scarce outside, or when sugar is fed, or when the sweet honey juice is accidentally spilled, the whole beehive becomes "boiled." This sudden sweet smell and a large amount of sugar stimuli can give the workers a sense of sense that a huge source of outside honey has exploded.

In a state of excitement, honey bees' collection instincts are magnified, and they start searching blindly for any possible source of sweetness. At the same time, guard bees, who are normally in charge of their guard, will lose their job because of the frenzy of the beehive as a whole, and will not be able to identify in a calm manner who are their companions and who are their visitors.

Search for bees for role and information.

Once an exciting worker bee or scout bee has smelled the radiant smell of honey and managed to steal a bee, he returns to his own group to dance "swipe." This intense dance will rapidly infect the entire bee and attract a large number of people to follow the odour group.

This crowd-excited looting is extremely contagious. It would have been just a little bit of an experiment with a few bees that would have turned into tens of thousands of wild attacks in just a few hours. Weakly protected hives simply cannot resist this organized wave of looting, and the nest door will suddenly be completely defunct.

How to prevent and respond to the bee-breeding crisis?

In order to prevent such an exciting disaster, beekeepers must be particularly careful when they need to feed or check during the season. The operation should be light and fast, and no sugar or honey should be spilled outside the beehive. At the same time, it would be appropriate to narrow down the nest doors of the beehives, make it easier for the guards to defend themselves and reduce the chances of invasion by outsiders.

If, unfortunately, a serious bee-jacking has taken place, the bee-keepers must take immediate measures to take the fruits, such as covering the nest door of the stolen beehive with wet cloths, closing all beehive boxes for a while, or using smoke and water to cool the frenzy of the bee-breeding, thereby breaking the bee-breaking link and protecting the peace of the entire beehive.

While the social fabric of the bee world is excellent, so is the competition for survival. Understanding the causal relationship between bees' excitement and bees' theft not only helps us to upgrade bee-rearing techniques, but also makes us more awe and understanding of nature's wonders. It is hoped that this article will enable your fellow bees to avoid these potential crises in their daily management.
