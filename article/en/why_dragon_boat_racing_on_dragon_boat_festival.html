Why do you row a dragon boat at noon? It's an amazing story to open up a thousand-year-old custom.

Author: Ah Hau Date of issue: 26 September 2026 Update: 26 September 2026 Reading time: About 4 minutes

Every time we arrive at the fifth midday of May, the streets and streets are filled with festivities. In addition to the fragrances of the family members, the lasagna and the scavengers at the door, the most ferocious and shocking activity is definitely not "drive the Dragon Boat". And with the sound of a full-blown drum and the hollering of a single one, a gleaming dragon canoe rises upon the water. Have you ever wondered, however, why we're rowing the dragon canoe this afternoon? This is the legacy of a thousand years ago, and it is a deep historical and moving story.

The most well-known and well-known legend is that of the patriots of the time of war. Kuohara's life loyal to his country and an active advocate of innovation and unity against the Qin have been squeezed and framed by the traitors of China and even exiled by Chu. On his way to exile, he saw his country falling, filled with endless sorrow and anxiety.

It's a sad memory of history.

In 278 B.C., the Qin army stormed the capital of Chu and heard the news that the country had fallen. On May 5th, he wrote "Whisa" on the same day, with a stone in his heart, he marched into the river and wrote about his country's last loyalty and mourning.

The locals were deeply saddened and frustrated when they learned of Kyuhara's suicide. In order to save this noble man, we have fought to find him in the river of Zoro and hope to find a miracle. At the same time, people were concerned that the shrimps in the river would chew on the remains of the prairie, so they threw the rice herds into the river, hoping to draw the living creatures out of the water. These self-mobilizations of the year turned into an important tradition of the midday canoes and the eating of stings.

The older source: the dragon of the 100th people.

Although the legends of the memorials are widespread among the population, according to the in-depth tests of many historians and archaeologists, the actual origin of the dragon canoe was much earlier than it was. In the ancient south of China, the 100th Viet Nam ethnic group inhabited the children of a self-declared dragon. They live in the water, and they're good at the canoe and the water.

For the ancient people of Viet Nam, the agricultural calendar was viewed as a "bad month" of climate change and disease, full of natural disasters and threats. To pray for the safety of the people and for the good weather, they will make a dragon-shaped solo. The canoes, which perform great rituals of sacrifice and competition in the water, entertain the gods of water and dragons, and at the same time serve the purpose of shunning evil and sending evil away.

The cultural spirit of the Dragon Boat.

As the time has passed, the rituals of rituals that were originally part of the southern clans have gradually merged with the legends of the patriots of Central China, giving the tradition of the Dragon Boat a deeper spiritual dimension. It is not just a pure water contest, but it brings together more respect for historical figures in Chinese society, a legacy of ancestral traditions, and a good desire to protect their homes and pray for peace.

In the modern world, the Dragon Boat is beyond national boundaries and has evolved into an international fine sports event. When the race begins, the drummer at the bow must master the rhythm and sound the drums like a dragon's heart beats; while the rudder at the tail is in charge of the direction, ensuring that the pen is straight forward; and the mid-tens of the oars must be full of attention, at the same pace and show the unmatched team's solidarity and commitment.

A thousand years of water symphonies.

From the blessing offerings of the ancient people of Viet Nam, to the search and rescue of the river that was launched by future generations in memory of Kashihara, and to the current international competition, which has brought together strength and beauty, solidarity and struggle, the dragon canoe culture has been baptized for thousands of years, still showing its strength and unique charm.

Next time you're watching the Dragon Boat competition on the edge of the river at noon, listen carefully to the sound of the deafening drums and the shout of the other side. It was not just a race of speed, but also the cultural memory and spiritual cohesion shared by hundreds of generations over the centuries. Let us celebrate this great tradition and hope that this water symphony will continue in the future for generations to come!
