Why do kids have to go to school? Ninety-nine percent of the parents are silent, and 1 percent of them are sent directly to their children!

By Mina Date of issue: 24 November 2025 Update: 24 November 2025 Reading time: about 6 minutes

"Why am I going to school? When 99% of parents say, "Because you have to read!" "I hate writing!" "Why do I have to get up early every day?" "I don't want to go to school!" and "I don't want to go to school!" But did you really think about what would happen if you really let the kids go to school? Many people think it's just for a test or a diploma, but the real point is much more. Today's article is not about preaching, but about 10 of the most realistic reasons -- why do children have to go to school? After reading it, many parents were silent, and they sent it directly to their children, saying, "Look for yourself!" because these words are more useful than the 10,000 times we shout, "You have to read." Ready? Let's go.

Reason 1: School is the only place a child can legally waste time.

Sounds counterintuitive, but it's true. Only in schools can children spend years trying, playing, making mistakes, making friends, falling in love, joining a club and spending the night preparing for events. Out of the society, you've never been so low on the wrong cost. A mistake may cause you to lose your job, lose your money, lose your trust. But at school, the teacher said two words to you, and you'll be there in a few days. This protected time is the most precious respite in life. Many adults look back and find out that it's true freedom in the student age. If I miss it, I'm never going back. So, school is not a waste of youth, but the cheapest price for the most valuable growth experience. That's always worth more than any workshop.

Reason 2: The school teaches you "to be with someone who doesn't want to be with you."

The most painful part of the job is not hard work, but hard work for colleagues. But do you have a choice? No. The school is the best rehearsal. You'll meet a hateful classmate, a tough teacher, a bully, a kisser, a bunch of weirdos. You have to learn how to be with them, how to protect yourself, how to express dissatisfaction and how to gain power. These "social muscles" have been forced into school for decades. When you get into society, you don't break down because you first meet a loser. Many people think that they can stay away from human relationships, but simply change the field from school to a job. And the job is worse. The school is the hangout training camp that life gives you.

Reason 3: The cheapest entry ticket in your life.

You may have heard a lot of stories about a certain boss country that graduates and earns money. But it's the survivor's deviation. The reality is that 99% of the good jobs, and the first level of the short run is studying. No college degree, no interview. Some say, "Capacity is more important than a degree," yes, but you have to have a chance to show it. The diploma is the ticket that brought you in. And the cost of reading is really low: nine years of compulsory education in the country is almost free of charge, and universities spend tens of thousands of dollars a year, divided into four years. It's a knock-on brick that can be used for life. If you don't read, you're lucky to turn around; if you don't read, you have to choose. The choice is always worth more than a single way out. This is not a snob, but a cruel reality.

One more thing is important: schools teach more than knowledge and teach you how to learn. This power will follow you forever. The world of the future is changing too fast, and today's popular business may disappear in 10 years. The only thing that won't pass is "learning how." It's the most invisible superpower the school gives you. A lot of people only found out when they were adults: the worst exams, reports, readings, were actually helping you to practice. It's too late to regret. So now the kids are crying to go to school, and they won't be crying to go back. Take that, please.

Conclusion: To go to school is an opportunity for future self to not regret.

It's normal for a child to hate school because it's tired, bitter and annoying. But believe it, it's the shortest time in life. It's nothing compared to the pain of being an adult and not reading. It's not for parents, not for teachers, but for the future 30, 40, 50. Let him have more options than just bowing down. If one day your child asks you, "Why should I go to school?" Please forward this article to him. And tell him: 'Cause it's the feeling that Mom and Dad want you to cry, not regret.' Reward. And I'd like to welcome the parents and make more children understand that.
