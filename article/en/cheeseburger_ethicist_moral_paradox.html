The double standard behind the cheeseburger's scientist, even his peers!

Author: Handsome. Date of issue: 26 September 2026 Update: 26 September 2026 Reading time: About 8 minutes

It's a irony that thousands of ivory towerists feel their faces beat. In our daily lives, our Prime Minister certainly believes that those experts who spend all their days studying ethics and writing great books of great and noble science must also be private saints of high moral character and perfect behavior. However, reality is often even more absurd than novels, and a new term popular in philosophy in recent years, "cheese Hamburger" , is unwittingly exposing this huge lie.

The term is based on the observations and ideas of well-known philosophers, and is used to describe professional and ceremonial professors who are generous on the podium, talk about animal rights and the moral imperative of veganism, turning around without hesitation and enjoying delicious cheeseburgers.

What's a cheeseburger for a scientist?

According to Eric's research and discussions at the University of California's Riverside Philosophy Professor, one often wonders: is a professional lycée more ethical than the general population? The answer might break your glasses. Studies have shown that their moral behaviour is not much higher than that of ordinary people.

These scholars are fully aware of the suffering caused to animals by the industrial chemical livestock industry, as well as of the moral controversy over meat consumption and the environmental damage caused by carbon footprints. In theory, they have more reason than anyone to change eating and eating habits; but in fact, their appetite is associated with the habits of social groups, allowing them to compromise.

This mental state of "I know it's wrong, but I'm going to eat it" is typical cheeseburger science. They do not understand morality, but they show the collective and highly consistent weakness of human beings in the face of temptation.

Can moral knowledge really change behavior?

This led to a deeper soul torture: What is the point of philosophical thinking if you study the rules of ethics every day, but you can't agree on your daily behaviour at all? Are years of learning and learning just a high-level intellectual gymnastics that has nothing to do with real life?

Many believe that the more reason is understood, the better. But in the cross-section of psychology and philosophy, scholars find that the human brain is good at cutting "know" and "get it done." They can write a logical, impeccable paper that proves that a certain way of life is immoral, but when they starve, the primitive desire of the brain quickly takes over.

This situation is not confined to eating and eating. In terms of academic malpractice, the proportion of charitable donations and daily hospitality, the performance of professional leading scientists is often just “just as successful” as usual.

How do we see this awkward truth?

In the face of the "cheese-hanist" situation, we do not have to completely deny the value of philosophy. On the contrary, this honest self-censorship is where philosophy is fascinating. It tears naked the disguise of human rationality and reminds us that there is always a gap between moral ideals and real life that cannot be filled.

A truly healthy philosophy of reflection never pretends to be a perfect saint. It encourages us to face our own hypocrisy and insufficiency and to acknowledge the extent to which we are able to endure the distance from the ideal.

The next time you see a big philosopher in the middle of the night talking about the truth of the universe, eating double-story cheeseburgers, don't be too surprised. This is just the most real and vivid picture of humanity.

This debate about the difference in knowledge is far from over, and is it possible to narrow the gap between theory and reality in future research in the field of ethics?
