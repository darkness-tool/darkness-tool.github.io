Why do eggs and ducks see each other every day, and they're so big that few people eat them?

What's the secret behind this?

To understand this situation, we need to look at the characteristics of this animal from the production end. There is a popular saying called "one chicken, two ducks, three geese" that vividly depicts the rate of egg production in different poultry. In comparison to the fact that hens are almost able to produce their eggs in good breeding conditions, and that the egg rate of the female ducks is very high, the female geese produce not only a small number of eggs, but also a strong season.

Low production and high farming costs

A healthy hen can produce two or 300 eggs a year, but a she-goose can often produce only a few dozen eggs a year. In addition to this, geese have a long life cycle, a large body size and an amazing diet, and demand for space is much higher than for chickens and ducks. This means that the costs of cooking geese are very high and the field costs. For the farmers, the main economic value of the geese is meat and the acquisition of feathers, and the eggs become an unstable by-product.

In addition to low production, transport and storage is a major challenge. The large head of a goose egg and its thick eggs are fragile, and in the present logistics transport it is difficult to withstand the pressure of stacking, with a high rate of damage during transport. At the same time, the thicker airholes of the goose eggs, the loss of moisture and the short-term security period have made many traders reluctant to take the risk of high losses to sell the goose eggs.

Why don't consumers pay too much?

In addition to the difficulty of making a lot of purchases on the market, the consumer's own eating habits are a key factor. One goose egg tends to weigh two or three eggs. Another reason why many people are being held back is their taste and taste.
