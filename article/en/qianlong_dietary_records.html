What did the Emperor eat in his food file?

Author: Big Man Date of issue: 8 September 2026 Update: 8 September 2026 Reading time: About 8 minutes

Turning through the heavy historical records of the Forbidden City, there is no greater victory than the "Bookbook" that records the Emperor's daily consumption and occupation! In the imagination of many, the Emperor, the king of a kingdom, must be filled with hundreds of mountains and precious seafood every day. However, what is the real Qingming Palace food like? Through the vast files kept in the palace, we have been able to look at the most real daily face of the Queen.

The Qing Dynasty Emperor's daily diet in the palace is strictly regulated and usually consists of only two regular meals a day, different from breakfast and dinner, and a mix of snacks or midnight snacks, as required. The breakfast is usually provided in the morning, while the evening meal is provided in the afternoon. These meals are run by the Royal Dining House under the Department of Internal Affairs, and are being carefully prepared by the top cooks and eunuchs.

Two meals a day and a very rich vegetable structure.

According to historical reports, the Emperor's daily diet, though abundant, is by no means an aberrant 108 dishes. In general, each meal consists of dozens of hot, cold, small and many staples and soup. His habits of eating and eating and feeding, his staples being made of pasta and rice, and his meat being dominated by chickens, ducks, pigs and sheep, show the perfect integration of Manchuria tradition with the Chinese food culture.

The King's exclusive appetite for food.

In the hundreds of meals in the dry mountains, later people discovered a very interesting phenomenon: he had an unparalleled love for the duck. Whether it's a petling duck hot pot or an octopus of all kinds of cooking, it appears in his daily menu. In addition, as a result of repeated patrols in the South, the Gangnam food system, like Su-Hang, has been deeply integrated into the palace's food, and has injected fresh and purified colours into its already rich and lavish taste.

In addition to meat and valuable supplements such as twigs, dry table foods are often found at home. It's like fresh cabbage, spinach, tofu, eggplant, etc. These seemingly ordinary foods have grown into a delicious, taste-rich, balanced, warm-natured diet in the hands of the chef.

Strict food safety and complexity.

The Emperor's food procedure is extremely extensive and heavily guarded. Every time a meal arrives, the eunuchs quickly set up tables, chairs and utensils, and present the royal meal one by one. Before the Emperor's chopsticks, the next-door eunuchs must "eat their meals" and every course must be equipped with a drug-testing silver medal to ensure that everything is safe. This highly institutionalized process reflects both the royal supremacy and the high level of vigilance within the court.

It is worth mentioning that the Emperor's meals are often carried out alone, without the companions on the side, unless there is a special reward or a celebration. It was also a high political honour at the time that after the end of the meal the leftovers were given to a beloved bride or a good minister according to the rules.

From the menu, the mystery of longevity.

From the age of age to the age of eight, the menus of the Emperor's lifetime foods are constantly adjusted, mostly based on soft, digestible, and stylish foods. The fact that he does not drink alcohol, does not eat badly, does not eat well, and does not eat well, perhaps is the key to his ability to live a high life and to become the longest and longest-lived emperor in Chinese history. These precious historical records still tell the world endless court legends.
