You know your MBTI?

Author: Editor-in-Chief Date of issue: 1 June 2025 Update: 1 June 2025 Reading time: About 3 minutes

The MBTI is a tool to reveal character! In this diverse world, everyone has their own unique character. MBTI divides these characteristics into 16 different types, helping us to better understand ourselves and others. Each of these types has its own particular advantages and challenges, and through this article you will be able to learn more about the characteristics of these people and find the most appropriate ways of working and human relationships.

MBTI is classified according to four opposing characteristics: outward (E) and inward (I), intuition (N) and perception (S), reflection (T) and emotion (F), judgment (J) and perception (P). These four sets of features combine 16 character types: ISTJ, ISFJ, INFJ, INTJ, ISTP, ISFP, INFP, INTP, ESTP, ESFP, ENFP, ENTP, ESTJ, ESFJ, ENFJ, ENTJ.

What's your character type?

Each MBTI type has its own unique characteristics. For example, INFJ-type people are often perceived as comprehensible and perceptive, while ESTJ-type people are known for their decision-making and organizational capacity. The knowledge of your character type helps you not only to find the right role in your profession, but also to improve human relations and communicate more effectively with others.

In understanding MBTI, you might find yourself in a certain quality of preference. These preferences do not mean that you can only behave one way, but rather provide a framework to help you understand your behaviour patterns and the driving forces behind them.

How can MBTI be used to enhance self-awareness?

Through MBTI tests, you can better understand how you react in your work environment, how you make decisions and how you interact with others. This will not only help individuals to become more efficient, but also promote team cooperation, as understanding each other’s characteristics can reduce misunderstandings and conflicts.

MBTI and career choices

The role of different MBTI models in the workplace varies. For example, an INFJ-type person may perform well in psychology or social work, while an ESTJ-type person may exercise his or her leadership skills in management or executive roles. Knowing your type helps you to choose the career path best suited to your personality.

The MBTI in social life.

MBTI is not only used in the workplace, but also in social life. Knowing the character type of a friend or family helps you to better understand their behaviour and needs and to strengthen relationships.

Finally, remember MBTI is a tool, not an absolute label. Everyone is unique, and the diversity of character allows us to be creative and adaptable in different environments.

Conclusions

By learning about MBTI, you can not only improve your own understanding, but also your interaction with others. Explore your character and use it to shape a better life. That's the way everyone works!
