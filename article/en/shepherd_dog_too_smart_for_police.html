Super smart sheepdogs can never be dogs? The back cause is sore!

Author: Keung Date of issue: 23 November 2025 Update: 23 November 2025 Reading time: About 7 minutes

For many years, border sheepdogs have been recognized as the world's smartest dogs, but they hardly see them in dog vests? If you ask any dog expert, "What kind of dog is the smartest?" The answer is almost entirely border shepherd Collie. They can remember thousands of words, they can open their own refrigerators, they can even identify hundreds of different toy names, and IQ completely crushes other dogs. However, the presence of border sheepdogs is rare in the dog corps of all countries around the world, and the main dog is always the German shepherd, the horse dog, and Labrador. Why is that? Aren't they strong, fast and fierce? The answer is the opposite -- they're too smart to be dogs! When the truth came to light, even the trainers could wake up. Because the "smart" of the sheepdog and the "obligation" that the dog needs are different things. They think, question, refuse to follow "unreasonable" instructions, which cannot be tolerated in the dog community. Today is the day to uncover the cruel truth that makes dog-lovers sore.

The dog needs "absolute obedience," not "independent thinking."

The senior dog trainer stated that, while the dog was on mission, the guide dog had to do exactly what he had to do and not allow half a second to hesitate. For example, a drug search dog must immediately sit down and warn that he cannot run himself to remove his luggage. The same can be said of the dogs, who found the explosives only to mark their location and cannot be touched out of curiosity. But border sheepdogs are natural sheepdogs, and the job for thousands of years is to "stand alone" how to get the flock to the right place. The master needs to go down in a big direction, and it's up to him to figure it out. The genes of "independent thinking" are engraved in DNA and can't be turned off. For example, when you say "Sit down," he looks around and says, "Is it really reasonable to sit down?" If he thinks there are more important things in the environment (e.g. sheep running away from home), he may simply ignore instructions to deal with "more important things." This behaviour is a genius at the ranch, but it is directly considered to be "disobedient, obsolete" at the canine training grounds. One conclusion: The dog wants a robot, but the shepherd is a self-aware Einstein of the dog community.

The true case: the story of a brain-drained nasal acid.

In Taiwan, there was a border shepherd named "Flash" who was trained by the dogs. In the first month of his first grade, the instructions were learned more quickly than the German Pastor, which surprised the trainers. But the second month started having problems — every time he smelled drugs, he didn't sit down to warn, but pushed the stash through his nose to the trainers. The trainer punished him, and next time he picked up the bag with his mouth, put it at the trainee's feet, sat on his tail and waited for praise. The trainer was so mad, "I told you to sit down and warn. Why do you have to find a way to solve the problem?" Flash turned his head like he was saying, "Isn't it faster?" How can you be so stupid? "The smartest dog in the whole room has been kicked out of his training because of "too much opinion." The trainer said in private: "For the first time in 20 years from the police, I felt helpless about a dog who didn't need us at all. On the day of the retreat, Flash took the training vest back to the locker, like, "This job is not for me. I'm leaving." He was later adopted by a shepherd and now commands hundreds of sheep every day on the farm, better than being a dog. And every time the trainer talks about it, he chokes: "It is not good enough, we are not worthy of his intelligence."

The best thing to do is to...

In fact, border sheepdogs are not unemployed, they are just doing their higher-level jobs. It's like a search dog (which needs an independent determination of what to do with the ruins), a world champion of a flying dog (which needs an instant calculation of angles and speed), or even a therapeutic dog (which can detect changes in human mood). There is also a growing number of border pastoralists who are "dogs for hearing impaired people" and help to distinguish the number of sounds, telephones, alarms, etc. Their cleverness finally found the right stage, instead of being asked to become a "mechanism that only obeys orders". So the next time you see the edges on the plane and catch the airway, remember to applaud it — the dog was fired by the canine team for being too smart, and is now the world champion. It's never been able to be a police dog, but it's never too proud to be a "do what you want" dog. True genius, always choose to be himself. You know what "too smart to lose" dog stories?
