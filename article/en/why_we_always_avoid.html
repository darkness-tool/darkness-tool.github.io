Shake! Why are we always running away? Psychologists reveal the hidden truth and make you understand!

Author: Treasure Date of issue: 28 December 2025 Update: 28 December 2025 Reading time: about 6 minutes

Did you ever ask yourself: "Why do you want to escape when you're in trouble?" In a busy modern life, many are often under stress, and in the face of work, human relations or internal conflict, the first reaction is to delay, divert attention or even escape altogether. This is not laziness or weak will, but the psychological defence of the human brain. The psychiatrist Joseph Burgo, in his book 'Why do we always run away?', states that all psychological defence is a lie we lied to ourselves to escape pain. While avoiding can bring short relief, long periods of time will only lead to more problems and more anxiety and suffering. This paper will provide you with an in-depth understanding of the psychological causes of the escape, the usual type of security system and how to break this vicious cycle. After reading this article, you will rediscover your hidden emotional map and learn to face and live a better life. It's not just psychology, it's the inner circle of many. Let's open up this mystery that's haunting hundreds of people. Are you ready to face yourself? This in-house exploration tour is about to begin.

Human beings are born to experience pains like anxiety, shame, anger or grief. When these emotions are too strong for us to bear, the brain automatically activates the defense to protect itself. This is the core concept of Freud's psychoanalytical theory, further developed by its daughter, Anna Freud. Defense is like an invisible barrier that helps us avoid suffering for a while, but also prevents us from seeing what we really need. The common reasons for flight include fear of failure, fear of rejection, fear of internal conflict, or past trauma. For example, when we encounter criticism at work, we may choose to deny the existence of the problem or project it on others. These actions have reduced pressure in the short term, but they will prevent us from growing up, or even getting deeper. Psychologists point out that evading strengthens the negative cycle because the problem has never really been solved. Understanding these mechanisms is the first step towards change.

What are the usual mental security systems?

The following are the most common forms of psychological protection in modern times: 1. Denying: refusing to admit painful facts, such as knowing that a relationship is wrong, but insisting that everything is good. 2. Repression: Pushing uncomfortable feelings into the subconscious and pretending that it does not exist. 3. Projectivation: To blame others for their negative feelings, such as "all your fault" when angry. 4. Rationalization: to justify behaviour by creating excuses for failure. Escaping: directly avoiding situations that cause suffering, such as delaying important tasks. 6. The ostrich mind: Like the ostrich in the sand, pretending that problems will not happen. These mechanisms, while protecting us, are too dependent to prevent emotional experience and self-esteem. Many clinical cases show that prolonged flight can lead to anxiety, depression or human relations problems. The experts stress that the pain itself is not terrible, but rather the second harm we have inflicted in order to resist it. It helps us to be more conscious in choosing to face, not to escape. Next time you try to escape, ask yourself: What's behind this?

To avoid behaviour is to be seen everywhere in everyday life, for example, when you brush your cell phone in the face of a deadline, when you know that there are health problems and you are not treated. Psychological studies show that evasion is a kind of negative force: it feels better to avoid being uncomfortable, so the brain learns to repeat it. But it'll make snowballs more and more anxious, as the problem is delayed and the pressure accumulates. Many think that running away is lazy, but rather a fear of potential suffering. Like fear of failure, choose not to try; fear of intimacy, push the relationship away. These patterns are often the result of childhood experience or social teaching to suppress negative feelings. However, accepting all emotions, including pain, is the key to freedom. As the book says, learning can be more productive with pain. Many healing stories show that when faced with bravery, people often feel relieved and grown up. You can practice from childhood and break the escape habits.

The consequences of flight and social reflection

Long-term flight may affect not only individuals, but also human relations and society around them. For example, evading responsibility in the workplace can damage the team; evading communication in the family can fuel resentment. The current society is under a lot of pressure, and many people are avoiding reality through shopping, games or social media. These short-term comforts have left the heart empty, creating a vicious cycle. Psychologists have called for attention to mental health and learning to be emotional. Some people have succeeded in removing their defences and rejuvenating themselves through therapy or reading. There's a community discussion. Someone's sharing. They missed the opportunity, and some said they faced a change in life. This reminds us that avoiding is instinctive, but the choice is the beginning of growth. Many people are beginning to look at emotional education and want to help more people escape the shackles. Such reflection not only enhances individual well-being but also enhances social cohesion. Maybe it's just a way to get away from what's coming to us.

How to break the cycle of flight?

The first step in breaking escape is to see their own defense. I'm trying to document the emotions and thoughts of every escape and find a pattern. And then, practice the mood: allow pain to exist without trying to control it. Starting with a small target, we have to face things of fear, such as a daily delay. Seeking professional help, such as CBT, can effectively change habits. Learning can also help you stay with your heart and reduce the need to escape. Remember, it doesn't mean a few seconds, but it's moving forward. Many share, unguarded, and feel the ease and strength that are unprecedented. And you can be the brave one you're facing. Let's take this step together!
