Why are you shouting "Heng ONG HUT" all over New Year's? Discovering the super-treasure secret behind this!

Author: Yuki Date of issue: 24 January 2026 Update: 24 January 2026 Reading time: About 3 minutes

As soon as the New Year of the Farmer's Day comes, it becomes a national slogan! Every time we enter Malaysia or the Chinese community in Singapore during the New Year of the Farmer's Year, whether we shout at each other when we live, congratulate each other when we celebrate, or commercials and TV shows, the words "HENG ONG HUAT!" are heard everywhere as if they were the brightest background music of the New Year. Where does that come from? Why do we have to shout? Today, let's learn more about this lucky-nang blessing language!

First of all, "Heng ONG HUAT" is actually the pronunciation of the Chinese language (Foujan, Hokkien) and the Chinese language is "hing-fong". The three words are clear: "Heng" stands for prosperity, prosperity and good fortune; "Ong" stands for prosperity, excitement and business; and "Huat" means "richness," which is everyone's favorite meaning, "richness."

Together, "happiness" is the perfect combination of "prosperity, wealth, and the city of Bliss." The Chinese are the best of all in the year, a short, strong and loud sentence, and a new year’s wish, which we all want: a successful career, a financial boom, and a rising flame.

Why does the New Year of Agriculture have to say that?

The New Year of Agriculture is China’s most important holiday in the year, and we hope that we will move on and wipe out the bad luck of the past for the best. So, from New Year's Eve to the whole month, whether it's a reunion, a meal or a life-saving ceremony, all sorts of good words are repeated, and "Heng ONG HUAT" is almost a slogan because it's too classic, too direct.

This is particularly popular in the Chinese society, especially in Malaysia and Singapore, where many of their ancestors come from Fujian and Guangdong, where Hokkien is the main dialect. In contrast, the northern Chinese may say "congratulations for wealth" more often, but in Southeast Asian circles, "Heng ONG Huat" is the rule!

The classic scene of life: crying out hiss!

The best use is to feed! "Heng AH! ONG AH! HUAT AH! Go all the way!" The louder you shout, the higher you get, the more you get, the more you get the New Year's fortune, the better you get. This custom holds up the good fortune, the higher the wealth.

In addition to being born, seniors give red bags in their New Year's birthdays, and they say "Heng ONG HUTAH!" to express thanks and blessings. Businessmen often write "hing-hong" or use the word "single" at the door to beg for business to flourish and to get lost.

The origin of this is cultural.

"Heng ONG HUAT" is from the traditional lyric of the Yinan language, and the Chinese culture has always had the tradition of "pronouncement" that good words bring good luck and bad words bring bad luck. In the New Year's era, however, you are particularly reluctant to say "lost" and "dead" and "break" and instead to say "fast", "fing" and "hing" in your mouth. Over time, that phrase became the most representative blessing of the new year.

Interestingly, there are young people who would play the sound barrier, say "Huat Ah!" alone, or add a "lah" to "Heng ONG HUAT LAH!" and add a more intimate sense of humor. In online times, the phrase often appears in meme, in short films, in New Year's songs, like the Malaysian singer's classic New Year's song, named Wee, and it's well known.

Anyway, "Heng ONG HUT" is not just a word, but a New Year's message. It represents the Chinese eagerness for wealth, prosperity and good fortune, as well as the spirit of optimism inherited from their ancestors. Whether you're from Fujian or any other Chinese, you can't help but say the last thing you want to say:

Remember to cry out for the New Year of the Farm and let good luck come to the door! May the New Year be a happy one, a rich one, a good one!
