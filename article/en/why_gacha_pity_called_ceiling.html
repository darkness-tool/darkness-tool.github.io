As long as there are players who have been playing card-picking mobile phone games, the word "sunhole" is no stranger. However, for a new friend who has just touched the world of hand, it is inevitable that a huge question will be raised in his heart: why is it called "the sky well" in Japanese and in the inner language, which represents the roof of the house?

To understand this interesting term, we must first go back to the construction and traditional culture of Japan. In Japanese, "sunhole" means "ceiling" or "top" of a house. It's a very graphic idea to borrow it on a card game. When a player throws money into a card pool, it pulls cards over and over again, like stacking up, and the rule is the maximum cost or the limit, the unbreakable ceiling on the top of the head.

The history of the blood and tears of Japan's handstroke.

This is a term that can be widely disseminated and used as a common language in the world, dating back to the famous "Grambalue Fantasy" by Cygames in Japan. In that year, a very rare and powerful role card was introduced, which led to the issuance of thousands of crazy player cash cards. There was a Japanese man who hit a lot of people all the way to get this card. A hundred thousand yen is still in the fold, provoking huge social debate and player questions.

The incident shocked the authorities and left players strongly dissatisfied with the lack of transparency and the lack of bottom-up financial system. In an effort to calm the anger and comply with the relevant legal regulations, the authorities have subsequently introduced a new system of exchange. The system provides that, when a player earns a certain number of points in the same pool (e.g., the famous 300) and no matter how bad the luck ahead and how many other proprietors they draw, the system will either send out or allow the player to replace the current lead.

Why is it "the well" instead of "the floor"?

A lot of people wonder why Japanese players choose the "sunhole" in their instincts, since it guarantees the minimum rights. In fact, the term describes the "extreme limit of the upward accumulation of the amount or amount spent." You can't go up when your cards go up like balloons, and you can't go up on top of the ceiling, and you have to stop here and force the reward. As a result, the top limit is portrayed as the "sunhole".

For a long time, Chinese players have been using the Japanese word directly in their exchanges. Even in the day-to-day conversations of many players, a unique measurement unit such as "one well" appears. For example, when you talk about the cost of a new game, you say, "It's a game where you spend about a well in order to graduate," meaning that you have to reach the maximum number of times set by the ceiling in order to be able to secure your role.

A small difference between a well and a general base.

Although in everyday life, the terms "sun wells" and "bare bottoms" are often considered synonymous, there is a delicate difference between the two if the definition of old players is to be taken into account. The traditional "soils" are often a cumulative point system that is independent of the pace of the cards, with points exchanged directly after reaching the given number of times, while the narrow "basket" is a system that triggers rare items when a given sample is available.

However, as the trade industry continues to evolve, the rules for smoking cards introduced by various manufacturers become more diverse, and the line between the two words becomes blurred. Now, whether it's for the exchange of coins or for the delivery of a given drop, it's common practice to call it a well or a bottom.
