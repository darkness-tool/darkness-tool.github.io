Super crazy! Why do you have to hold on 24 to 48 hours? If you don't stay put, you'll ruin everything!

Author: Ah Hau Date of issue: 14 January 2026 Update: 14 January 2026 Reading time: About 8 minutes

This is definitely the biggest secret weapon in Clarillo's bakery! For the first time, many people were trying to get into the oven, and then they got the failure of a shell that collapsed and had a dry inside like a cake. In fact, the point of all this is that the pasta must remain silent for 24 to 48 hours! This seemingly extra step is a decisive factor in the creation of the Creiro brand, the shell and the wet embroidery core. Without static, the bread in the paste is still working, making it easier to bake with bubbles, to wear a tatter, and even to prevent the skin from burning into perfect caramel. The professional baker unanimously stressed that the more static time was approaching 48 hours, the closer the finished product was to the extreme taste of the classic Bordeaux.

The clitoris, although not very high, still contains breaded protein. Freshly mixed pasta, the bread is in a very active state, full of bulletproof and constrictive force. If baked directly, pasta swells at high temperatures, resulting in an uneven surface bubble, or even a "backside" or collapse. After 24-48 hours of static, the pasta is relaxed, the pasta is stable, and it is only when it's warm and baked that it expands upwards, forming the classic cylindrical look and crust. Many of the bakers share it, and if they skip the silence, the products tend to look ugly and have a bad taste.

The magic of flour co-operation.

The static process allows flour to fully absorb liquids such as milk, egg fluids and complete water co-operation. This not only makes the pasta softer and smoother, but also releases natural yeast from flour, helps to reassemble the flour and protein and enhances the stability of the whole structure. Many experts have pointed to the lack of static pasta, the uneven distribution of water and the vulnerability of large holes or bottoms to baking. On the contrary, with the full silence of the pasta, the internal organization will present a symmetrical beehive, like the very strong Castile Brie, whose mouth is damp, and whose teeth cannot stop.

In addition to structural changes, static allows for the full integration of the scent. The fragrances of vanilla, rum, etc., take time to penetrate the pasta. It's a long time to eat, and it's a long time to eat. And the baker said, “The more she hides, the more she waits, the more attractive she is.”

24 hours, vs. 48 hours. What's the difference?

It's been good for 24 hours, but it's Q-balling and chewing. By 48 hours, the pasta is fully ripe, the crust will be thicker and more condensed, with deep caramel velvet; the insides will become so tender and so delicate as a butterbread. A lot of bakers have found that the 48-hour version of the whole mouth is clearly upgraded, with a much better surface and a much better shape. Even though 24 hours is acceptable, 48 hours is the king's path if you want to pursue the extraordinarys of the classic Bordeaux. Of course, more than 72 hours of static may have overstretched the pasta, leading to a collapse and suggesting the best time.

A lot of freshmen wonder if there's a quick version of Clarillo. Indeed, there are formulations that try to shorten the static time by using high-weave flour or ice-water bathing techniques, but the finished product tends to be less than perfect, less thin and less fragrance. The traditional French-style approach insists on keeping quiet for a long time, precisely to ensure that every detail is in place. When you wait patiently for the time when the noodles are ripe and then you get into the hot oven, you'll find everything worth waiting for!

Still small skills and common errors

Silence must be sealed to freeze, to avoid the skinning of pasta or to absorb the aroma. The best effect is to expand before the baking is removed, so that it is evenly mixed (but without excessive gas) and then calmed back to the room temperature. Among the most common errors are: too short a time of silence, which leads to impurities in the skin, over-composed production of violent bubbles, and lack of oil in the molds, which causes sticky molds. As long as you've got the key to static, with the right level of baking, you can easily make a Collulu at home!

In conclusion, the 24-48 hours of silence is not an absence, but a secret at the heart of the Clillo success. It relaxes the bread, the flour and the flavors, and eventually produces the addictive shell and wet core. Next time you do Clarillo, remember to give pasta enough time to rest -- because good things are worth waiting for!
