The Inexhaustible Secrets of Exploration of the Universe: The Tremendous Evolution of Astronomy from the Old Star to the Modern Astronomy

By Jade. Date of issue: 8 September 2026 Update: 8 September 2026 Reading time: About 8 minutes

Astronomy is one of the oldest and most fascinating natural sciences in human history. Since ancient times, when humans looked at the stars and tried to understand the changes in seasons and time patterns, this science has carried with it an endless curiosity about the unknown world. Retrospect of the evolution of astronomy is not just a breakthrough in science and technology, but a brilliant revolution in human thinking from mythical superstition to rational truth.

In the early days of civilization, ancient Babylon, Egypt, China and Greece conducted long and orderly observations of the night sky. China’s ancient astronomers have documented a great deal of daily, monthly and comet data for calendar and agricultural production, even leaving the first supernova eruption in world history. Many astronomy at this time is linked to time measurements, sacrifice to the stars and the close direction of survival.

From the earth's heart to the sun's heart's turn.

In ancient Greece, scholars began to try to explain the universe with rational and geometric models. Toolemi presented the famous "heart of the Earth" that the Earth was at the center of the universe and perfectly dominated all the world's life. This theory has dominated the Western world for over 1,000 years. However, with the increasing complexity of the data, this complex system has finally led to radical changes.

By the time of the Renaissance, Polish astronomer Nicholas Copernicus had the courage to put forward the Sun, saying that the sun was the centre of the universe, and that the Earth and other planets were orbiting. Galileo then directed the newly invented telescopes to the stars, witnessed the profits and losses of Jupiter's moon and Venus, and supported the heliocentric system with hard evidence, laying a solid foundation for recent astronomy.

Modern physics and modern cosmology. Fan.

Following Kepler's discovery of the three major laws of planetary movement, the great physicist Newton proposed a law of gravity that successfully combined the physical laws on the ground with the sky and declared the golden age of classical astronomy. Since then, humans have not only been able to see stars, but have been better able to calculate their past and future.

In the 20th century, astrophysics ushered in explosive flight. Einstein’s broad-based relativity provides a whole new space-time perspective, while scientists such as Haber have observed that the universe is expanding and that it has given rise to a “big explosion theory” that has rocked the world. Spectrographs, wireless astronomy and the intermingling of modern space telescopes have brought human vision from visible light to a whole new era of electromagnetic spectrum and even gravitational waves.

The evolution of astronomy is evidence of the great light of human rationality, from the light of the star in the eye to the edges of the deep black hole and the detection of radiation from the cosmic microwave background. Every technological innovation has brought us one step closer to the truth of the universe and has led us to redefine the place of human beings in the great stars.

In the future, with the development of a new generation of giant telescopes and deep space exploration techniques, the secrets deeper in the universe will gradually be revealed. This never-ending journey of interstellar exploration will continue to inspire the imagination and the desire of all human beings to lead us to the ever-greater stars.
