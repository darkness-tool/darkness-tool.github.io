Why can't you use your finger on the moon? It's amazing what happens when the elders warn you of the truth!

By May. Date of issue: 13 August 2026 Update: 13 August 2026 Reading time: about 6 minutes
HERO FIGURE

This is a taboo that almost every Chinese child has ever heard of! On a clear night, when the silver moon shines on the earth, the children stretch out their fingers to the moon in the sky, and they are put to their knees: "Don't let the moon be with their fingers, or their ears will be cut off!" This phrase follows the childhood of countless people, which makes the moon both respectful and fearful. Where does this taboo come from? Will there really be gods to cut ears in the middle of the night? Or is there a more rational explanation behind it? Let's start with this long-held wisdom.

According to the legend that has been circulated, pointing your finger at the moon is a big disrespect to the moon god. The moon is considered sacred in the Chinese culture and is referred to as a superstar or a moon-mother, a typhus, etc. The ancients thought that the moon was filled with gods, pointed their fingers at him, as rude as they pointed their fingers at people, and even angered him. When the Moon is angry, he cuts out the offended person ' s ear in the middle of the night, and the next day he wakes up with red ears, cracks or cuts. In some places, if the moon is not carefully pointed out, and as soon as the moon is greeted with sincere apologies and prayers, it will be possible to seek forgiveness and abdicate punishment.

The ancient origins of taboos can be traced back to Qin and Qin. Song Da.

This taboo is not new, but has deep historical roots. China's ancient days have been filled with awe of the sky and its elephants, with the fear of pointing their fingers directly to the sun and moon. In the poems of the Qin era, "The East, the East, the East, the East, the East, the East, the Qin." No one dared to use it with their fingers when it was to the east because it was disrespectful to heaven. By Song Dynasty, the Book of the Dow had more clearly listed a number of taboos, including spitting on a meteor, using a finger rainbow, using three fingers without moving (the sun, the moon and the stars), and staring at the sun and the moon for a long time, which were considered to be disrespectful to the gods and would be punished. Three lights represent the light of the sky and are considered sacred at random.

In Taiwan and in the southern parts of the country, the taboo has become more "as if the moon will be cut off." The Taiwan Folklore published in 1921 mentions that the moon is said to have a sharp knife, and a child can be stabbed in the ear if he curses it or speaks bad. In the Taiwan Folklore of 1969, it was clearly stated: "No finger-moon, no moon-to-moon, will be cut off by the moon." If you are not careful, ask for forgiveness with your hands. As you can see, taboo content has evolved over time from curse to finger, punishment has changed from cutting to more severe cutting, and rules have been added to apologize for impunity. There are similar songs or rituals in Jiangxi, Fujian Zhenzhou, Yongjiang, Zhejiang Dongyang, Chongqing, etc. If a child fails to point to the moon, he must sing a specific song for forgiveness of the moon or apologize for it.

Why "slice your ears"? The link between the moon and the sword.

Why does the punishment fall on the ears and use the "cut"? Most of the explanations are related to the shape of the moon. When the moon shows the upper or lower string, the bent moon teeth look like a sharp blade or knife. The ancients saw this shape, and naturally thought of the "slice" movement, so they made up the story that the Moon God would cut his ears with this knife. Adding to the tender skin of their ears, they are prone to red swelling or cracks, which coincides with the point of the moon, and are thought to be causal. In addition, the use of fingers is itself an impolite act, and intimidation is particularly effective in order to teach children a habit of respect, especially when it comes to sacred and common objects like the moon. The kids hear their ears cut and they're used to it.

This taboo has also been heightened by the religious worship of the moon in the Chinese society. The moon is sanctified to be a nymphoma, a symbol of sanctification, procreation and reunification. It is the tradition of the mid-Autumn Festival and the observance of the Moon that continues. The ancients have limited knowledge of the astronomical world and are mysterious and awe of the evolution of the sun and moon, so many taboos and rituals have developed. The moon with its fingers is seen as an insult to the moon God, as if it were an insult to the idol. In some places, it has even been argued that if a child has an ear problem, it is necessary to worship her or her sister to heal, further strengthening the credibility of taboos.

Scientific truth: It turns out that he's making a mistake.

Although popular legends are interesting, modern medical views provide a more rational explanation. Many vowed that they had pointed to the moon when they were young, and that the next day there was a wound or a terrible pain in their ears, and that the memories were not fiction. However, the real cause is not the punishment of the moon, but the common heterogeneity of children, known as rashes. The heterogeneity of the skin tends to develop in the early stages of the child, with the risk of relapsing, especially in the thinner parts of the ear, face, neck, bend of hands and feet. In the past, poor hygiene conditions made children vulnerable to allergies, climate change, scratches or bacterial infections, causing injuries in the night, which coincided with the time of the moon, and were associated with the idea of "ears cut by the moon."

Doctors have pointed out that when a child is warned to point at the moon, he/she is afraid to use his/her hands to touch his/her ears to make sure there are no injuries. Young children often have sweat and bacteria on their hands, which, together with skin sensitivity, make it easier to inflammate or increase rashes. When they grow older, become immune and live in an improved environment, the symptoms of this kind of disease are naturally reduced, and the taboo "demonstration" is left in childhood memories. As a result, there is no direct causal link between the red slits in the ears and the moon itself. It is only a coincidence of time, coupled with the strengthening of civil beliefs, that the legend has been transmitted to this day.

What is the point of this taboo in modern times?

Although science has explained the true causes of ear injuries, the taboo "no finger moon" still has its cultural value. It reminds us of a fear of nature and tradition, and also of the clever way in which elders teach children manners. The use of a finger to point a person at a person is truly rude, and it is more effective to behave through a specific and mysterious target, the moon, than by mere teaching. During the Mid-Autumn Festival, many families still remind their children not to use their fingers for the moon, both to perpetuate folklore and to add to the festivities. Whether you believe in the Moon or in medicine, the legend reflects the deep attachment of Chinese society to the moon and its wisdom.

Next time you look up at the bright moon, think about this story, which has been transmitted from Qin to the modern world. You can choose to put your hands together and salute them, or you can put your fingers to the moon, because what really matters is understanding the culture and science behind you. The moon is still high in the night sky and silent evidence of human curiosity and awe of the universe. This taboo may no longer be feared, but it always reminds us that respect for nature and curiosity about tradition is the best attitude. Were you warned when you were a kid?
