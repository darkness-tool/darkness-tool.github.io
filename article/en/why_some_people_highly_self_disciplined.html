Surprise! Why can some people be super-regular? The secrets of science turn you into a self-declared man!

Author: Daji Issued: 1 January 2026 Update: 1 January 2026 Reading time: About 8 minutes

Have you ever admired those super self-disciplined people? They get up early, they keep reading and they finish their work on time, and they wonder why they can do it, and I'm always done halfway. In this fast-paced era, self-regulation seems to be a successful proxy. Many try to foster self-regulation by forcing themselves to do so, but often fail. In fact, some people are highly disciplined, not just by their will, but by scientific reasons. From psychological learning to neuroscience, studies show that self-regulation involves multiple factors such as genes, habit formation, brain structure, etc. This explains not only why some people are born to be more disciplined, but also give us the hope of changing. Let's explore these amazing discoveries and reveal the true secrets of self-discipline!

First of all, self-regulation is closely related to the forehead of the brain. The former leaves are responsible for decision-making, planning and impulse control. The study found that self-regulating people are more active in their front leaves and can better resist temptation. It's like the brain's command center is stronger, making it easier for them to make rational choices. On the contrary, if the frontal leaves are less functional, people are vulnerable to immediate happiness and are unable to sustain long-term goals. Psychologists point out that this part has a genetic element, and that some people are born with a higher level of self-regulation as a child.

Genetics and environment: the secret of natural self-regulation

Scientific research shows that self-regulation has a partial genetic base. The twin study found that the genetic rate of self-regulatory quality can be over 50 per cent. Some people have specific genetic variations that make it easier for them to develop habits and maintain power. In addition, the environment of childhood is crucial. It is easier for children to internalize these values if they grow up in a regulated family and parents stress their responsibilities and responsibilities. On the contrary, excessive drowning or chaos may weaken self-regulation. This explains why some people have been "cruise" or "sports" since childhood, while others need to work the day after tomorrow to catch up.

The power of habit: self-regulation is not driven by will. Here.

Many have mistakenly assumed that self-regulation depends on willpower, but psychologists have found that the true high-level self-discipliner is “self-driving” through the habit of growing up. Once behaviour becomes a habit, there is no need to deplete willpower. For example, those who get up early do not struggle to get up every day, but are used to waking them up. The famous marshmallow experiment shows that children who are able to delay their satisfaction become more successful when they grow up, which is related to their good habits. The study suggests that, starting with a small target, with only one push-up per day, growing, it can become a self-regulating habit, making life easier.

Also, internal motivation is a powerful driver of self-regulation. When you really like something, or you know its long-term benefits, self-regulation becomes natural. On the contrary, external pressures alone (as others expect) are often not sustainable. Successful people, such as athletes or entrepreneurs, often have a strong sense of purpose, allowing them to enjoy the process rather than suffering. It also explains why some people are able to work out or learn to be super-push, because they get a sense of achievement and happiness.

How to become a self-regulatory? The advice of science.

The good news is that self-regulation can be nurtured in the day after tomorrow. Psychologistics suggests that small and specific targets be set, that the environment be avoided (e.g., delete the tempting App) and that progress be tracked to achieve a sense of achievement. At the same time, ensure adequate sleep and nutrition, as willpower is like muscle, and fatigue is easily exhausted. Many of the high-level self-regulating people have a regular schedule, starting day after day, to put the brain into an efficient mode. In the end, forgiving yourself occasionally fails to learn from mistakes rather than giving up. Over time, you will find that self-regulation is no longer a hard job, but a shortcut to freedom and success. These scientific methods have helped hundreds of people change their lives, and you can do it!
