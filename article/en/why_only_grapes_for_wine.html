Why is it that only grapes in the world are used to make wine? The rest of the fruit is lost to it!

Author: Tiger. Date of issue: 18 September 2026 Update: 18 September 2026 Reading time: about 5 minutes

Human civilization has evolved for thousands of years, and the history of alcohol fermentation is almost in step with civilization. We can see apple, plum, blueberry and even banana wine in a world full of fruit, but when it comes to the word "wine", the vast majority of the world's businesses are all about grapes. It's curious. Is it true that only fruits like grapes have the potential to become wine? In fact, behind this is a tens of thousands of years of natural evolution and scientific coincidence.

To understand this situation, we have to start with the core principle of wine — yeast. The essence of yeast is that yeast consumes sugar and converts it to alcohol and carbon dioxide. Theoretically, as long as a juice with sugar is given a chance to be a wine, why is it the grapes that come out and become the world's absolute master of wine? This is inextricably linked to the natural and perfect gift of grapes.

Born with a pass: the secret of a natural yeast and a bark.

First of all, there's a huge advantage hidden on the fruit skin of grapes, which is natural yeast. If you look carefully at mature grape surfaces, you find a layer of white frost called "polls." This powder is actually filled with hundreds of millions of wild yeast. When grapes mature on the ground or are crushed and broken, the internal juices interact with the yeast of the skin, and nature automatically activates the fermentation process, with little need for humans to add any extra bacteria.

In contrast, many other fruits also contain sugar, but their skins or structures are not as natural as grapes with wild yeasts for alcohol fermentation. If other fruits are placed at random, it is often easier to produce fungus or to decomposition directly, without being able to easily convert into a well-stable liquid.

The incomparable gold-gold-gluct and snub structure

Apart from the natural yeast, the chemical composition of the grapes is as much as it's made for wine. Producing good and long-lived wine requires enough sugar to shift the desired alcohol intensity and, at the same time, enough acid to contain the growth of harmful bacteria. The glucose concentration of grapes is among the highest in fruit and their own pH values are usually in high acidity between 3 and 4.

This unique "gold glucose acid ratio" not only makes yeast work happily in a safe acidic environment, but also effectively prevents bacterial infections. Even better, grapes andseeds contain rich symphonies that provide a complex sense of substrates and structures for wine, a balance that is difficult to achieve with strawberry, watermelon or citrus fruits.

Why aren't all the other fruits used for wine?

Maybe someone would ask, "Why can't other fruits make wine?" In the case of apples, for example, apples can make cider, but the acidity of apples is different from the stingy structure of grapes, and the taste of alcohol is one-on-one and unsatisfied over a long period of time; and berries such as strawberries or raspberries, although they are fragrance, are too watery and less acidy, and direct fermentation often requires a large amount of sugar and artificial acid to be forced into alcohol.

In addition, like fruit rich in fruit and glue (e.g. plums or some pears), it can easily generate large quantities of methanol or sticky matter during the construction process, which is difficult to handle. And citrus-like fruits, with too high lemonic acid and unique oil components, interfere with the normal operation of yeast and fail to successfully complete high-quality alcohol fermentation.

Life in the barren land: the survival philosophy of vines

In addition to chemical and microbiology advantages, grapes show amazing advantages in farming. The vines are very resilient plants that particularly like to grow on steep slopes that are poor, rocky and even unsuitable for growing food crops. On the other hand, a fertile land will give vines leaves and dilute the taste of fruit.

The ancient humans found that the collection of grapes, which are grown on barren land and are not easily used as a daily staple, can be used for a period of time as a secret, long-lived, mouth-to-mouth yeast that brings pleasure. It's a perfect combination of time and time, so that grapes quickly take over in the wine stage of human history.

In conclusion, grapes are the only fruit in the world that is used in large amounts of large amounts of alcohol, not by chance for humans, but by nature’s most perfect configuration of sugar, acidity, yeast adhesion, and plant adaptability. For the next time, when you're going to be fine, you can thank nature for this perfect fermentation that has evolved over millions of years.
