That's not a crime in criminal law. Legal experts are stupid!

Author: Arrested Date of issue: 24 November 2025 Update: 24 November 2025 Reading time: About 9 minutes

It's definitely something to show you! In a world of seemingly strict laws, the Penal Code contains numerous offences, regulating order and justice in society. However, have you ever imagined that, in this vast body of law, there are certain charges that have never been actually committed since they were enacted? These "crimes that have never been committed" are like ghosts hidden in the jungle of the law, making it hard for legal experts to shake their heads. Today, let's talk about the stories behind these strange charges and why they're so "lonely". Perhaps this is not only an interesting story of law, but also a reflection on the limits of human behavior and the legal prognosis. Can't it be strange to imagine that a law that has existed for decades has never worked? In fact, this is not uncommon in criminal law around the world, especially in Taiwan and China’s legal system. Their existence reminds us that the law is not only a tool of punishment, but also a proactive design. Are you ready? Let's open the mystery veil together!

First of all, we have to admit that human imagination is sometimes far less than that of legislators. In the Chinese Penal Code, there is a new known crime: the specific deformation of the crime of forgery of the seal of the State apparatus. According to discussions in the legal forum, this type of offence, although explicitly prescribed, has never been prosecuted for technical and opportunity constraints. Similarly, in U.S. law, only one person who has been charged with perjury in a state-level tax claim has not been convicted since its inception. These charges are like "relays" of the law, always prepared and never activated. The transfer to Taiwan’s criminal law, although there are no absolute “zero cases”, has almost never taken place in some rare cases, such as the extreme circumstances of the offence of endangering aviation security. The expert notes that this reflects the stability of society and the existence of a human moral base. But there are also concerns that, if technology improves, these charges will suddenly "awaken"? In any case, their existence makes criminal law more comprehensive. It makes us wonder: is the law too much to predict the future?

What do legal experts think?

In his interview, Professor Lee, a well-known law professor, said: “These crimes, which have never been committed in the criminal law, are in fact measures taken by legislators to prevent potential risks.” He cites, for example, that in article 185 of the Taiwan Penal Code, certain deformations, such as "a flying vehicle endangers public safety", have never been practicable. Because in reality, people's behavior has not reached that extreme. Another lawyer, Ms. Wang, added: “Think about it, the crime of counterfeit currency may have been common before digital times, but some high-tech counterfeiting methods have not been tried yet. She laughed that it was like the law was waiting for the first brave man. These experts agree that, although no one has ever committed these crimes, there is a need for their existence. They not only fill legal loopholes but also educate the public about taboos. At the international level, there are similar situations in Japanese criminal law, such as certain environmental crimes that have never been triggered. This has created a common understanding among the global legal community: prevention is better than cure. But there are also questions as to whether these "free" laws should be abolished to simplify the Code. Professor replied: Better to prepare than to use.

In more depth, a classic example comes from China: there have never been a defendant for some spy-related offences. It's because the international situation is stable and the technology is high. In Taiwan, article 109 of the Penal Code, which has altered the form of treason, such as "financing outside the country" , has been regulated, but no one was involved in it in peacetime. This type of crime shows us the two-faceted nature of the law: strict on the one hand and much more on the other. "If someone did, wouldn't they be the first in history?" This imagination has led to a lot of discussion and even to jokes about "challenging" them. But the expert warned: don't try, no law. The existence of these offences has also prompted legislation to review the law on a regular basis. Perhaps in the future, with the rise of AI and space technology, new charges will arise, but the old ones will sleep.
