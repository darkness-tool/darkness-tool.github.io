Why do you have three different brands for the same dress and color? The owner doesn't want to tell you!

By Jade. Date of issue: 28 December 2025 Update: 28 December 2025 Reading time: About 9 minutes

It's a mystery that many streetwalkers have encountered, and it's confusing you! In a dress shop, did you ever look at a beautiful shirt or pants, rushing to pick up the same color, and discovering that the name on the placard was different? Why would there be two or even three different numbers? Is it the store price? Or is it a fake? In fact, this is a common phenomenon in the garment industry, with professional inventory management and production reasons behind it. We're here today to reveal this secret, so that you don't get confused about the next time you go shopping, but instead you become an insider! The same clothes, the same colour, were used to produce multiple shipping numbers, mainly from production batches, poor dyes and supply chain demand. These differences, while difficult to discern, are of paramount importance to shopkeepers, avoiding storage confusion and marketing problems. This is not only the case with large brands, but also with many wholesale markets and chains. When you understand, you'll find the world in costumes so detailed! Next, let's step by step to decipher this amazing story.

First of all, the most common reason for this is the fact that there are different batches. Clothing is usually done in multiple groups, especially in the form of hot sales. Different batches of fabric come from different times or from factories, and even with the same colour, the dye can vary slightly. In order to divide these batches, the factory will give a different number (SKU). So that the store can track which of the imports, which sales, and avoid confusion about the stocks. If the customer returns the goods, it will be able to determine exactly which product. This is more common, especially in fast-fashion brands, because of the high frequency of feed. Looks like the same clothes, which may actually have a slight colour difference or a different feel in fabric. And that's why the insiders pay special attention to the numbers.

The chromosome and quality controls are the key.

The second important reason is "chromosomal transition." When the fabric is dyeed, even when the same formulation is used, light colour differentials can be caused by different cisterns or weather moisture. These colored eyes are sometimes not visible, but they can be found in strong light or more. In order to maintain consistency in quality, the brand will mark different batch numbers. This would not only be internal management, but would also prevent clients from complaining about different colours. Some high-end brands are even renumbered to make sure they're perfect because of a slight color difference. In addition, differences in fabric providers can lead to nuance differences, such as thin veils or changes in weavers. This makes it necessary to have multiple numbers in the same colour. For consumers, it's actually a protection mechanism to ensure that the best match is bought. Next time you see a different uniform of the same colour, choose the favorite one. This also explains why there are times when old and new prices are different, and they're hidden behind them.

The third reason is "the need for storage and sales management". The modern clothing store uses the SKU system, and each variant needs a unique number. Even if the color is the same, if it comes from different import channels or from seasonal supplements, there will be a new number. This will help track sales data, supply time and promotion strategies. For example, a new batch after the sale may have different costs and prices adjusted. The chain of stores is more in need of a one-size-fits-all system to avoid the disruption of national stockpiles. This is also common in wholesale markets, where manufacturers distribute goods to different numbers. For the boss, the multi-carrying is a good way of calculating profit and risk. Consumers sometimes find a discount on the old number, the original price of the new number. You know, when you go out to the streets, you're smart enough to choose the highest price.

How do consumers cope with the multi-cartel phenomenon?

In the face of the same color, many consumers are worried about fakes or defectives. In most cases, it is normal business practice, not a problem. But if you care, you can ask shopkeepers why the numbers are different, and usually they explain the batch or the time of arrival. It is suggested to compare the material and to check the consistency of the hand of the fabric with the colored velvet. If it's too different, then consider whether to buy or not. When the insider buys clothes, he picks first the number in the hot market because the quality is stable. In addition, online shopping often includes multi-cartels, and attention is paid to the evaluation and return policy. Some brands explain the code on the Internet to make consumers more comfortable. This also reflects the professional nature of the garment industry, from production to sales ring. The next time I meet, I'm not surprised, but I can share this little knowledge with my friends. Get to know the insides, make you a smart fashion consumer. Maybe this is another kind of fun shopping!

Conclusion and follow-up

The secret of the suit number shows us the fine management behind the industry. It's complicated for consumers, but it's for better quality and service. Next time you go shopping, pay attention to the tags. You'll find more interesting details. Maybe there's a couple of "twigs" in your closet. It's not just a little puzzle, it's the tip of the iceberg of the fashion world. If you have similar experience, you're welcome to share your findings! We'll continue to track more clothing and shopping. Keep your eyes open and bring more surprises! Let's be smarter shoppers together!
