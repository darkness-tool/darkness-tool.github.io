Why do I always find it hard to trust people? Psychologists reveal the truth!

Author: Yuki Issued: 1 January 2026 Update: 1 January 2026 Reading time: About 3 minutes

Do you often feel that there's a wall inside that makes it hard for you to really trust people? In human relations, trust is the cornerstone of relationships, but many people always feel that they cannot be fully open. There's always a hidden sense of defence, whether it's a friend, a partner or a colleague. It's not just "personality" but it's often hidden behind it. Psychologists point out that the difficulty of trusting others usually stems from past traumatic experiences, and that we build protection walls in our subconscious. This feeling not only affects intimacy but may also lead to loneliness and anxiety. If you have a similar problem, the article will give you insight into the causes and practical advice. Many lack security from childhood, making it difficult to trust others as adults. This is a reality that is becoming more common in modern society and that deserves our attention. It is through understanding the root causes that we can gradually break this invisibility barrier and regain confidence in people. Let's explore. What's going on?

First, the most common reason is the impact of childhood experiences. Psychologist Eriksson’s development theory suggests that if the baby is not steadily cared for by the primary caregiver, the "basic mistrust" model will emerge. When they grow up, this sense of insecurity extends to all relationships, making people suspicious of the motives of others. Many have strong control over their parents, who have been misjudged from an early age, and who do not believe that they can accurately assess whether others are trustworthy. This internalized negative belief can intrude even when faced with good will. The child's knowledge of trust is also undermined by the dual message of the original family or lack of honest communication. These early experiences have taken shape, affecting human interaction throughout our lives. Understanding this is the first step towards change.

Past betrayal: the pain of broken trust

Another reason is the treachery of adults. Whether it's cheating in love, the leaking of friends, or the calculation of jobs, if trust is seriously damaged, it leaves a deep scar. Psychological studies show that people tend to magnify negative experiences and ignore positive interactions. This is called "negative bias" and makes us pay more attention to the "harmed" case and underestimate the beauty of trust. When betrayed, the brain enters a defensive mode and sees trust as a risk. This fear will spread to new relationships, even if they are innocent. As a result, many people chose to isolate themselves and avoid being hurt again. But for a long time, it only deepens the sense of loneliness. Experts suggested that reconstruction should start from a small step, for example, trusting something that is not important. Time and positive experience can heal these wounds. Remember, not all of us are going to do it again.

In addition, low self-esteem or mistrust are key factors. If you don't believe in your value, you wonder why people are nice to you. This is often the result of a low level of childhood or a lack of certainty in the progression. Theories say that people who are anxious and dependent are particularly vulnerable to a lack of security in relationships and are always afraid of being abandoned. It's insecurities, it can project on others, making trust difficult. Psychologists point out that mistrust of others is sometimes a protective mechanism to avoid exposure to vulnerability. But over-protective, missing a link. Learning self-assertion is the key to improvement. Since childhood, you have believed in your own judgment, which gradually extends to others. Professional consultation can also help to clarify these patterns.

Untrustworthy expression and influence

In daily life, it is difficult to show that trust is suspicious, controlled or always independent. I don't want to depend on others, afraid of disappointment. This attitude, while protecting itself in the short term, has long brought isolation and pressure. The human relationship has become so thin that it is difficult to build deep ties. Research shows that people in low-trust environments are more likely to be anxious, jealous and even psychologically abused. In the workplace, it can also affect team cooperation and promotion opportunities. As a result, many are caught in a vicious cycle, the more they are isolated and the more they are. In community or online discussions, it is common to see similar harassment sharing. Let's reflect. Should we give him a chance? The expert calls for the building of faith, starting with the observation of predictability and dependence. Change takes time, but it's worth trying.

How to rebuild trust?

The good news is that trust can be rebuilt. First, start with self-confidence. To be sure of their worth, to practice small-step adventures, such as sharing irrelevant things. Seeking professional psychological counselling can help to explore the root causes and learn new models. "How can I trust you more?" "It's important to be honest. Pay attention to the balance of power and avoid feeling inferior. The day-to-day practice of honesty and commitment increases the sense of dependency. It is also helpful to read books or join support groups. Many people have become healthier through healing, trusting and trusting. Remember, trust isn't all or nothing, starting with small amounts. Be patient with yourself and change will happen. Finally, you'll find that open-up brings more warmth and connections. This improves not only the human person, but also the quality of life.
