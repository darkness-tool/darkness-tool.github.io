What's a line? The source and the basic definition.

The term "programming" is derived from Japanese and refers to a portrait of a person who is not a member of the CG, i.e., a stand-up or half-position stand-alone image of the character, usually with a transparent or monochrome background. Its central purpose is to perfect the character of the role, its configuration and its uniqueness.

In the case of "square" or scene illustrations that integrate people and the environment, more focus is placed on the role itself. In visual novels, word adventures, and card games of all kinds, drawing is the first step in shaping character flesh and charm.

The historical roots of painting: from tradition to modernity

If the original origin of the painting is traced, many scholars and amphibians believe that it is linked to Japan's traditional culture of “paper Zhiqing”. The show is a street drama, and the performers will tell the audience a story with a paper picture of the story.

At that time, people would paint the particular role of the appearance on paper, then cut off its edge, leaving a background blank, so that the performer would be more dynamic in the face of the scene. The way in which the role is cut out is in the shape of modern concepts.

Electron game-time push-backs

With the rise of early video games and word-risk games, the production team faced limitations in hardware and graphic space. In order to give the player a clear idea of who is talking and save time for drawing costs and file capacity in the course of the conversation, the developer began to use a large number of images of people in this context.

As technology progresses, simple static images evolve into a variety of expressions, differences in clothing, and even a dynamic technique such as Live2D, so that the painting is no longer just a dead-bed image, but rather a living, breathing, blinking life.
