We'll do it in the New Year! Rumours of truth!

Author: Da Ming Date of issue: 24 January 2026 Update: 24 January 2026 Reading time: About 8 minutes

Why do you have to put on a spring union and a firecracker this year? On New Year's Eve, family members used to hang lights, put on the Red Spring Union, and set firecrackers on fire. But you know what? These seemingly festive customs hide behind their backs an old and terrible legend — that is, to drive out the fierce “year-olds”! This legend has spread for thousands of years, giving millions a deeper understanding of spring customs.

A long time ago, the world was not as big as it was, and every year it was close, a ferocious and terrible monster would emerge from the mountains, called "years" and called "years". Its head is as large as a lion, its hair is dark, its eyes are red and its mouth is sharp as a knife. Every New Year's Eve night, it comes down into the village, devours livestock and even harms the villagers, leaving them in fear and suffering.

The horror of the beast.

The worst place for the animals of the year is where they are always silent and single-handedly launched on New Year's Eve. It is not afraid of knives and guns, nor of torches. The villagers have tried every way to defend themselves, but they have done nothing. Until one day, a mysterious old man with white hair showed up and told the villagers an amazing secret: the beast of the past year had three deadly weaknesses — fear of red, fear of loudness, fear of fire — so that it could easily run away.

The old man has further explained that red is the colour most feared by the young beasts, because it reminds them of blood and danger; the thundering sound of an explosion can shake. And its nerves make it fear; and the light of fire shines upon it, and leaves it untouched. The villagers opened up and decided to try the old man's way.

The history of the union: Red Exorcism

Then, on the New Year's Eve, the villagers took out red paper, and wrote on it blessed ties, such as "The heavens and the earth will increase their lives" and "Blessed Man's Life" and put it on both sides of the gate. The whole village turned into a red ocean with a big red lantern in front of the family. As soon as the beast arrived at the entrance of the village, he saw the red colour of his eyes, and was terrified and afraid to come forward.

Since then, Tsing Chun has become one of the most important customs of the year. The Spring Union is not just a decorative act, but a symbol of shunning evil and praying for peace. Red paper and black writings, with bright contrasts, keep the animals away from us forever. The modern Spring Union, though mostly in the words of blessing, is still a red red colour that carries with it the fear and wisdom of the ancients.

Origin of the firecracker: the sonic boomerang

In addition to the Spring Union, the villagers prepared a large number of bamboo canisters, filled with gunpowder, and threw them into the fire on New Year's Eve and made a huge bang. It's like a thunderstorm, and it blows all over the valley. When the beast heard the loud and deafening sound, he made his feet soft, and fled with his tail, and never looked back.

Then, people improved this way of burning bamboo into special fireworks, today's firecrackers. The firecrackers not only drive old animals away, but also signal the rebirth of old ones. The sound of the firecrackers is a sign of all the failures of the old year, a new year full of hope and joy.

The animals have been extinct since then.

After the New Year's Eve, the beast never showed up again. In celebration of their victory, the villagers came to each other every morning at the beginning of the day, saying, "Congratulations on wealth" and "Congratulations" in honour of the great victory of the retrograde beast. These customs continue to be the most precious cultural heritage of the Chinese nation today.

Today, while scientific times have made people no longer believe in the existence of a real animal, the tradition of springing and firing firecrackers has been preserved forever. They are not just the decorations of the holidays, but also the birth of the wisdom of our ancestors, and they remind us that in the new year we must drive out all difficulties with courage and solidarity, to a future full of hope. Let's think about this legend and let's celebrate the depth of one more culture.
