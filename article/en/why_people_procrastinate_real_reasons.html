It's not lazy! Psychologists reveal three real reasons why you can't get away with it. 99% of them are in!

By Mina Date of issue: 23 November 2025 Update: 23 November 2025 Reading time: About 4 minutes

You think it's just lazy? Every time you approach, you suddenly want to clean up your room, slide your phone, chase your head, or even stare at the ceiling. It's not your problem, and up to 95 per cent of the world's people are suffering from delays. Psychological research has found that the delay is not just a weak will, but a "self-defense system" of the brain to protect you from negative feelings. When a mission causes you anxiety, fear, boredom or uncertainty, the brain automatically labels "immediate action" as a threat, turning you to do something that immediately brings happiness. So, knowing that a report should be written, you can't help but do a three-hour short film. It's not that you're useless, it's that brain is "saving you." And what's even more amazing is that delays are addictive, because every time you escape successfully, your brain is rewarded with dopamine, which creates a vicious cycle. Today's article will reveal three of the most common, but most easily misunderstood, real reasons for the delay. After reading it, you will find that the delay is not lazy, but too smart. Ready? Come on, let's go. 99% of you got at least five. How many did you get? You can't say you're hopeless because knowing why is the first step to change.

Reason 1: Perfectism is the worst. Hands.

Many people think that the delay is due to the low standard, which is the opposite, and the more easy it is for the perfectists to delay. Because they fear that what they make is not perfect enough to meet their own standards. So the subconscious choice is not to start, so there is no risk of failure, at least the illusion that I could have done well. "Self-handicaping" is called a psychological excuse to protect self-esteem. Studies show that perfecters are 40 per cent more delayed than ordinary people. The "preparation starts" they often say is never enough. The more you want to get 100 points, the more you can't even start. Many of the high performers are also young with severe delays because they care too much about the outcome. The first step in breaking the procrastination of perfectionism is to accept "do it first, do it again." Remember: 80 points are always more valuable than perfect zero.

Reason 2: It's too big a job, and it's a direct brain.

The brain hates vague and ambitious tasks, such as "writing a book" "reducing 20 kilos of weight" and "learning English." Because there is no clear first step to this kind of goal, the frontal lobe will feel overwhelmed and give up. Psychologists call it "action paralysis." The solution is simple: cut elephants into small pieces and ask yourself, "What do you want to do next?" When you finish your first five-minute micro-action, the brain is entrified with dopamine, and you want to continue. That's why many people sit down and they can't stop, because the hardest thing is to start that step. Next time you delay, ask yourself: I'll just do it for five minutes, okay?

Reason 3: Fear of failure and fear of success

Many are afraid not of doing it badly, but of putting more pressure on doing it. For example, this is a great test. What happens next? This project is so successful. Will the boss give me more work? The subconscious felt that it was "safest to keep the situation safe" and chose to delay to avoid the consequences of a possible success. I am also afraid of being criticized, mocked and proven to be a bad person after failing. Under these two fears, the brain chooses the safest third path: nothing. Psychologists find that up to 70 per cent of chronic delayrs are also afraid of failure and of success. They say, "I don't care," but they really do. The best way to break this fear is to shift the focus from "results" to "transactions." Tell me: I'm just going to try. I don't want to be disappointed.

After these three reasons, do you still think the delay is lazy?

Procrastination is a signal that the brain is protecting you, emotional is asking for your help, not a moral flaw. When you know the real reason behind it, you won't use "I'm a jerk" to attack me again. Next time you want to delay, ask yourself: What am I afraid of now? Running from what? If you cut down on the task, lower the standard and allow yourself to do a bad job, you'll find the action to be very simple. Most importantly, the beginning is always more important than perfection. Let's start with the smallest action today, like closing the article now and doing the last thing you've dragged on for five minutes. When you press the start, the brain will thank you, because you finally understand its distress signal. You're not lazy. You just haven't found the right way. Now you know. Let's go.
