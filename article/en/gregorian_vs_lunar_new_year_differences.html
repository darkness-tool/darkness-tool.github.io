January 1st New Year, vs. New Year of Farming! What's the difference? Why does the Chinese world have two new years?

Author: Alvin Date of issue: 24 January 2026 Update: 24 January 2026 Reading time: About 3 minutes

Two new years are different! At the end of each year, on January 1st, people are going crazy to count down and set fireworks to celebrate the New Year, but it will not be long before they prepare for the year of the new farm year with red bags, a knitting union, and a roundup of suppers. Why would Chinese society, especially Taiwan, have two New Years at the same time? What's the difference between January 1st's New Year and the New Year of the Farmer? Let's decipher the origins, dates, customs and cultural meanings of these two festivals today, so you can understand them!

January 1st, New Year's Day: The New Year's Day

January 1st, New Year, or New Year's Day, is the first year of the Gregory calendar. This calendar, which dates from the ancient Roman era and was reformed by Pope Gregory XIII in 1582, has become a standard international calendar. The calendar is set at 365 days a year (366 days a year), based on a one-week rotation of the Earth around the Sun, and the date is thus stable and the new year is set on 1 January each year.

In the modern world, New Year's Day marked the beginning of global unity, with many countries, including Taiwan, setting 1 January as a national holiday. People will count down, watch fireworks, open champagne, make New Year's New Year's New Year's New Year's New Year's New Year's New Year's New Year's New Year's New Year's New Year's New Year's.

The Year of Farming: A deep culture of the traditional Spring Festival Root

The New Year of Agriculture, known as Spring Festival or New Year, is a festival calculated according to the traditional Chinese Sun Calendar. The calendar is dominated by the moon's short-lived calendar (about 354 or 355 days a year), while the sun runs to adjust the moon. As a result, the calendar is about 11 days shorter than the calendar, and the calendar dates for the annual spring season will fluctuate between January 21 and February 20.

The spring festival dates back to the time of proprietorism millennia ago, which was originally a popular legend for the "Yen Beast" and then became a major festival of sacrifice, reunification and exorcism. In New Year's Eve, New Year's Eve, New Year's Eve, Red Packs, Round-Up, Spring Union, Firecrackers, etc., are well known, highlighting family reunions, respect for the memory of the elderly, the desire for a new life, the desire for a smooth year, and the desire for a better life.

What's the biggest difference between the two new years?

First, the date is different: the calendar is fixed for January 1st, and the calendar changes every year. The next is a different calendar base: one is a pure solar calendar (sun calendar) and one is a sun calendar (sun-and-sun calendar). In addition to the way and the atmosphere of celebration: New Year's Day is one of modernism, internationalization and personalization; Spring Festival is traditional, family direction, respect for etiquette and ancestral worship.

In addition, the holidays are much longer. On New Year's Day, there is usually only one to three days' leave, while the Chinese Spring Festival usually has 7 to 9 days' leave, or even longer, in Taiwan, which gives us plenty of time to return to the country and to live in the spring.

Why does Chinese society have two new years?

This has to start with history. After the establishment of the Republic of China in 1912, Sun Zhongshan announced the adoption of the calendar, New Year's Day becoming the official New Year. However, traditional agricultural calendars have become entrenched and cannot easily be eliminated. The Chinese government moved to Taiwan, and Taiwan continued to do so: New Year's Day is the New Year's Day and the largest holiday in the tradition.

In Taiwan, while the Japanese were pushing for the new calendar, the Taiwanese were still sneaking over the old calendar and becoming a cultural resistance. After the war, it became even more apparent that two new years were still in existence: New Year's Day, which represented the modern country and international relations, and Spring Festival, which carried the family culture and cultural identity of Chinese blood. As a result, the Taiwanese "two years later" became a unique view, one official, one civil, one modern, one traditional.

Today, many Chinese, even outside China, celebrate the first of January in a simple manner, but not until the new year of the agricultural calendar. Buying, cooking, red buns because for them spring is the "real New Year"!

In conclusion, the New Year of January 1st and the New Year of Farming, while welcoming a new year, has a very different calendar, cultural significance, customs and emotional connection. It is for that reason that the Chinese world will have at the same time two meaningful new years, one to usher in a new global beginning and the other to continue a millennium of family kindness.
