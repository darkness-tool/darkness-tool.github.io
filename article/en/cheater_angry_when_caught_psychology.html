Surprise! Why do you always get caught cheating? The truth is stupid!

Author: Big Man Date of issue: 14 January 2026 Update: 14 January 2026 Reading time: About 8 minutes

This is definitely the dumbest image of a relationship! Imagine: when you finally took the proof of your partner's cheating, and questioned him with grief and sadness, and instead of apologizing, you were in a state of rage, yelling at you, accusing you of suspicion, or even falling on something. What kind of God is this? In fact, this "pre-emptive" reaction is very common in the case of an affair, and there are deep-seated psychological mechanisms behind it, so let's unmask this ridiculous veil.

Psychologists have pointed out that the first reaction when a defector is caught is often not remorse, but anger, which is in fact a powerful defensive mechanism. When their deep-seated sense of guilt and self-image is in serious conflict, the brain activates the perception of discomfort, and in order to ease this feeling of discomfort, they choose to pass on their emotions to their mates, pre-emptively provoke fire, and try to shift the focus away from their mistakes. It's like a mirror reflecting their inner panic and fear.

Perceptions of misalignment: the inner impulses that cheaters fear most. Twist

Acquaintance theory tells us that when a person's behaviour is inconsistent with his or her self-perception, there is a great deal of stress. The fact that the defectors usually consider themselves "good people" and "accountable people" has completely reversed this image. The moment they were discovered, when guilt and shame sprung like a flood, they could not bear the pain of self-inflicted destruction, so they instinctively wrapped themselves in anger: "You forced me!" and "You're too mad!" The pre-fire strategy was to protect the weak from their own mistakes.

A lot of people who have seen this show say that couples suddenly get angry. They suddenly turned from victim to perpetrator and even began to wonder if they were really too sensitive. The expert explained that this is precisely the smart transfer technique of the defectors: by attacking you and keeping you busy defending yourself, you have no energy to continue to pursue their guilt. This mood is psychologically referred to as " projection" and "reverse formation" and projectes the guilt of the heart into you, pretending to be the victim.

What does the expert say? Three tricks for psychological protection.

A number of relationship traders shared that more than 70 per cent of the defectors were found to have their first problems because of, first, fear of losing control (the secret life is broken); secondly, guilt is turned into an attack (the shame is masked with anger); and thirdly, attempts to regain moral heights (the accusation of mistrust, breach of relationships). A senior psychologist is more forthright, often following the DARVO strategy: denial, attack, reverse victim and perpetrator. When you're still heartbroken, they're already beating the shit out of you!

This "precipitation" not only makes the victim more confused, but also makes many people think that he is so angry that I may really blame him." But the truth is that most of this anger is a bluff aimed at delaying time, confusing attention and even giving you an apology. A lot of people in the community share similar experiences, and there are even people who almost give up on their lives because they are being yelled at to doubt their lives, and finally find out that they are having an affair. This kind of psychological warfare makes one wonder how powerful the survival instincts of the defectors are.

The way we reflect and respond.

In this case, do not be frightened by anger. Stay calm and gather evidence, which is the key to protecting yourself. The expert suggested not to get into a fight, but to make it clear: "We need to talk about the truth, not blame each other." It is also necessary to seek the help of professionals in order to really clear the way. The anger of the defectors is only the tip of the iceberg, and what really needs to be faced is the long-standing problems of both sides and the collapse of trust.

This "first-in-a-kind" trick tells us that betrayal is far more complicated than thought. Will there be a similar explosion next time? How can victims protect themselves?
