What's that? It's not like we're going to be able to find out what we're doing on the Internet.

Author: Haru Date of issue: 24 January 2026 Update: 24 January 2026 Reading time: About 4 minutes

It's a direct-wielding online phenomenon! In this time of social media, everyone wants to be seen. However, the means used by some to gain attention are often puzzling and even offensive. We commonly call this kind of person "crop."

What the hell is that?

The word "regard" is a combination of the word "concern" and "genue" in the language, which literally translates as "meth that needs attention". In a web-based context, it is often obfuscated to describe those who desperately want others to see and to seek a sense of existence. Such people often lack an inherent sense of self-satisfaction and have to rely on outside applause or debate to ascertain their value, as if they would disappear in the world if they were not noticed.

Four major behavioral features are common.

It's not really hard to see what's around them, they usually have very clear patterns of behavior. First of all, they like to be "piggy-eating tigers" or no sick moaning. It's a very small thing, and it's the end of the world through their mouths, or it's a stupid argument. Second, they're public performers. Quietly shouting in the library, or squeezing the topic during a dinner, just to get everyone's focus back on themselves.

Thirdly, they are very good at "cold water" or angry comments on the Internet, that is to say, "back to the north" or "lead to war" in the culture of "town people". They don't care about how people feel, they just care about whether the number of tweets or the number of messages goes up. In the end, there is usually a lack of common sense. When others share their joys or sorrows, they immediately turn to themselves, for example: "You have a cold?" I got pneumonia last month and almost died!

What are we gonna do about it?

Psychologists suggest that the best way to deal with this problem is "cold treatment". The core need is "regard" and "reward" for them, whether they are positive praises or negative insults. When you get angry, you fall into their trap. So, stay calm, don't give them any emotional responses, make them bored and usually make them feel uncomfortable. Moreover, it is important to build healthy boundaries and not let their emotional blackmail affect your life, but also to protect yourself.

In conclusion, knowing the term "specified" is not just an attack on someone but a reminder to ourselves. In the digital age of pursuit of attention, we also reflect at times, and are we, by accident, the one who lives for praise? True self-confidence comes from peace in the heart, not from the outside.
