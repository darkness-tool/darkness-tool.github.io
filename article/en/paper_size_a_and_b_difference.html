A book that understands the core differences and applications of paper sizes, series A and series B.

Author: Treasure Date of issue: 5 September 2026 Update: 5 September 2026 Reading time: about 6 minutes

In our daily lives and in our public environment, we are exposed to a variety of forms of paper every day, the most common of which is series A and B. However, many are often confused when asked where A4 and B5 are and why the smaller the numbers are bigger. In order to get to the bottom of these questions, we have to start with the ISO 216 standard set by the International Organization for Standardization.

ISO 216 is now the international standard of paper size for most countries in the world. This standard is backed by a fine mathematical logic, all of which have a long-width ratio of two square root equals one to four to one. This unique scale design is extremely convenient, and it makes perfect use of every inch of paper space, whether by magnifying or inflating a photocopy, without any loss of image or percentage.

Special features and daily applications for series A paper

The "A" series is the most visible and widely used paper in everyday life. For example, the size of the largest A0 sheet is exactly one square metre. When we turn A0 on the long side, we get two A1s, and then, like, a two-to-one, and then a two-to-one, you get A2, A3, A4 to A10. Of these sizes, A4 is the most central soul-player, with a size of 210 times 217 cm, and is used in a wide range of forms, printer forms, notebooks and various administrative documents.

In addition to the A4 that is common in the office, A3 sheets often appear in places that require double writing or graphic displays, such as project drawings or posters. Smaller A5 sizes are often used to make pocket notebooks, flyers or small manuals. Because of the intuitive nature of the A series, it is easy to understand the size and breadth of the relationship between the dimensions, provided that the concept of a trade-off is understood.

Definition and uniqueness of B series paper

The B series paper fills the gap between the sizes of the A series compared to the wide-ranging coverage of the A series. According to the standard definition of ISO 216, the size of the B series paper is the geometric mean of the A series paper with the same number and the first number. For example, B1 sizes are geometric averages of A1 and A0. This makes the whole size of series B a little bit broader than that of series A of the same numbers, with the same ratio at the square root of two, providing different visual effects and layout flexibility.

In real-market applications, we also need to note the small differences between the international ISO B series and the Japanese industry standard JIS B series. Most of the B5 or B4 photocopies commonly found in Taiwan and Japan use the JIS scale, which is 1 .5 times the size of series A. The B series paper, which is much broader than the A series, is well suited for the production of posters, book pages, maps and commercial printed materials requiring larger margins, so that the design layouts have more comfortable breathing space.

Summary: Series A and Series B selection key

In short, the most important difference between series A and series B is the size of the baseline size and the actual size. Series A, with A0 per square metre as its starting point, is a standard administrative document and daily printing option, while Series B, as an aid and extension of Series A, provides a perfect transition between dimensions, often in books, posters and specialized publications. The next time you choose a paper or a layout design, you can select the most desirable size, and you will not be disturbed by the size of the paper.
