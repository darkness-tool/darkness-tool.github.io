The moment you got off the plane, your head broke? The real change in "brain and air pressure" at the height of 100 m.

Have you ever experienced that when the plane was ready to land and slow down its height, stings began to occur between the forehead and the temple? Such a feeling of discomfort, which often occurs before and after the arrival of the flight, tends to give the false impression that it is due to a sharp rise in brain pressure. As a matter of fact, when people are flying on a plane, the internal pressure-modulation system is going through a dramatic trial that no one can see.

The main reason behind this is not simply a rise in brain pressure, but rather an imbalance between the pressure change, the pressure on the cabin and the human head cavity (e.g. nose and ear).

What happened to the air pressure in the cabin?

During the cruise, modern passenger aircraft usually fly at an altitude of 10,000 metres. In order for passengers to breathe normally, artificial pressure is applied in the cabin, but the pressure environment is approximately 2,000 metres above sea level, which means that the internal pressure itself is lower than the flat.

As the plane begins to fall and prepares to land, the air pressure in the cabin will quickly return from low pressure to constant pressure on the ground. This rapid pressure transition can have a direct impact on the various cavities of the human head, especially the air-filled nasal and osteal tubes.

Why do you get a headache when you get off the plane?

Medical research indicates that the headaches caused by the flight are related to multiple factors. When a plane falls and external pressure rises, if its own nasal mucular membrane is too allergic or cold, it leads to the opening of a nasal cavity and to a pressure on the outside.

This state of stress stimulates a trident neurosis, which leads to an expansion of the cerebrovascular veins or to the presence of pulsation headaches around the forehead, gills or eyes, commonly known as "systeal pressure" or "flying headaches."

How can we effectively prevent and slow flight?

If you want to be comfortable when you get off the plane, you are advised to do more swallowing, chewing gum or yawning when you take off and off the flight, thereby helping to keep the earpipe open and balance the pressure of the ear and head cavity. If there is a nasal inflammation or a severe nasal plug in itself, an angiogen spray or anti-iotamine can be used at the doctor ' s direction before taking off.

In addition, maintaining adequate moisture during flight, avoiding alcohol and caffeine-containing drinks, can help to reduce the sensitivity of blood vessels to pressure change. The next time you get on a plane, you can use these scientific methods to protect yourself.
