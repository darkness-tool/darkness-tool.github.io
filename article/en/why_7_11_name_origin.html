Discovery! Why not 24 hours?

Author: Ah Hau Date of issue: 24 November 2025 Update: 24 November 2025 Reading time: About 8 minutes

Have you ever wondered why the head of the global convenience store 7-11 is called that? In busy urban life, 7-11 Commissary has long been an integral part of our daily lives. Whether you want coffee in the middle of the night or catch a lunch, it's always available. But have you stopped to think about why its name is "7-11"? Is it because seven commodities sold to 11 bucks? Or some mysterious number game? In fact, it's a name about time. It's a surprise to hear it! Today, let's open up a long history of branding. From the ice shops in small towns in the United States to the global link to the Empire, the stories of 7-11 are full of coincidences and transformations. Ready? This is definitely a time to impress you with things you know!

It all started in the United States in 1946, when convenience stores were not as common as they are today. At that time, a company called Southland Corporation, which operated an ice and grocery store known as Tote'm Stores, was operating in the country. The shops were marked with a pedestal, and the customers left with the items they bought, known as "Tote'm". In order to expand the business, they decided to keep their names up and extend their business from the original short time to the opening from 7 a.m. to 11 p.m. This was a revolutionary change at the time, as most shops only operate during the day. The ad company Tracy-Locke then suggested "7-11" to represent this extended business period. It's simple, easy to remember, and it's a direct advantage for customers to know how to buy it later. It is not surprising that this name has so far been used as the world's most famous convenience store brand. Interestingly, the 7-11 was not a 24-hour operation, which took place only in 1963. A shopkeeper near Texas University who tried to open up all night to address the need for students to enter after the football match, which resulted in a surge in performance, opened up a front-runner for all-weather services.

History behind names.

Back to earlier, the forebears of 7-11 can be traced back to Dallas in 1927. At the time, the South Island company was a small ice supplier, which was later expanded to sell household goods such as milk and bread. After World War II, the US economy recovered, people's life picked up, and the demand for convenience surged. The decision to extend business hours is the key to capturing the tide. "7-11" is not only a symbol of time, but also a sign of the brand's promise to customers: to serve you anytime. Today, although it has been 24 hours since the closure, the name has been retained. In Taiwan, 7-11 is run by a single supermarket and has exceeded 7,000 since it entered the market in 1980. It not only sells commodities, but also provides services such as fees, parcels, etc., and becomes the second kitchen for the Taiwanese. But in any case, the name always comes from the golden period from 7:00 to 11:00. This also allows us to reflect that brand names often contain deep stories rather than randomly. The next time we move to 7-11, think of this history, perhaps another one.

Interestingly, the logo design of 7-11 is not clever. Why 7-Eleven, not full capital? It's said that the end of the n-case is for beauty, making the line more fluid. In the Chinese region, there is even a windwater theory that lowercase n can be "assembly". Despite official denials, the rumour adds much to the fun. In any case, the design has become brand-name icon, deep-rooted. From America to Asia, 7-11 adapts to local cultures, but remains loyal to the heart of convenience. Its success proves the power of a simple name.

Global Impact and the Taiwan Circus

Today, 7-11s have more than 80,000 home markets globally, the largest in Japan, followed by the United States and Taiwan. In Taiwan, it not only sells things, but also carries countless memories of life: coffee bought before the test, hot meals for hurricane night. The name comes from the United States, but it has taken root in Taiwan. "If it's 24-7, isn't it too long? 7-11 is very simple!” This also leads to a discussion about how brand names affect consumer awareness. Experts point out that numbers are easy to remember and have a rhythm, as in 7-11. In contrast, other links, such as FamilyMart, emphasize warm family feelings. But the numeric glamour of 7-11 is undoubtedly a classic case. In the future, with the rise of the electrician, how can the convenience store evolve? The name may change, but the spirit remains: from 7 to 11, to 24 hours of company. This story tells us that history is often more interesting than it looks. Next time I see sign 7-11, remember to smile, it has a story!

Or the "7 by 1" series of coffee, which is based on the insistence on choice. During the epidemic, 7-11 became a lifeline, providing masks and supplies. This has strengthened its indispensable status. The name is small, but it has a great impact, from 1946 to the present, and it's been over 80 years.
