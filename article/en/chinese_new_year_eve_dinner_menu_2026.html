What about the New Year's dinner? One of the most important moments of the year – New Year's Eve dinners are coming, and this is always the time of the year for many housewives or cooks: What are they cooking? How many dishes? What are the traditional, promising years of food? In fact, with a few core principles, it's easy to build a table where the whole family can enjoy a meal! In 2026, we came to help you draw up a well-used menu of New Year's dinners, so you can be confident from picking to table.

It's a tradition to have 8 to 12 dishes for dinner, in even numbers, in two pairs and two good things. The fragrances are all fragrances and, more importantly, the sweet meanings behind each dish, such as old age, high steps, and long lives. These messages not only make the table more ceremonial, but also give family full blessing and good luck.

The policy of the New Year's Dinner must be set: these dishes must be on the table!

First of all, the most important thing is "fish"! The symbol "over the years" is that the whole fish must come to the table and not be cut in a piece, preferably evaporated or burned red, to keep the fish intact. The next is the "whole chicken" and the "rooster" sound "home" in the Thai language, which represents a vibrant family and a family, usually made of white pheasant chickens or fragrance chickens. The third requirement is "long-term food" with mustard or spinach and a whole pot to pray for a long and healthy life.

In addition, Maroon foods, such as lion heads, goonballs, fishballs, etc., represent a conglomerate and a family. It's a hot choice. There's no less soup, a bowl of hot chicken soup or flaming walls that warms the stomach and the heart.

What about the little family or the lazy? Simple sharing of practices

If there are fewer people or not too tired, six to eight courses can be made: chicken soup with mushrooms, fresh fish, red steaks with electric pots, and a mix of curry paste, fragrance platters, lettuce salads and fruit dishes can be easily completed. Remember the small taboos of the New Year’s dinner: no porridge (meaning poverty), no fish cut (several) and no leftovers (which represent the lack of food in the coming year).
