When we talk about Mid-Autumn, the first reaction is often moon cakes, moons and family reunions. However, why is this festival called Mid-Autumn and often called Mid-Autumn? This is not just a word difference, it's a fine display of ancient Chinese calendars and seasonal ideas. The name of Mid-Autumn, from the ceremony of the Qin era, to the clear interpretation given by Song, contains a deep cultural code.

In order to understand why Mid-Autumn is called Mid-Autumn, it is necessary to go back to the ancient Chinese seasons. The ancients divided the year into spring, summer, autumn and winter four seasons, and each season was further divided into three months. The three-month divide is named after "Mong", "Sun" and "Moon" in order. # I'm not going anywhere # Meng represents the first month, the second month, the middle month, and the last month. This form of exclusion, in line with the "brother-in-law season" called "brother-in-law" by the ancients, has shown the importance of order and due process.

Three Autumn Scores: Mon Autumn, Mid Autumn, Mid Autumn

By the autumn, specifically, the agricultural calendar was called Meng Autumn in July, Mid Autumn in August, and Season Autumn in September. In August, the agricultural calendar was in the middle of the autumn, so it was called "Sun Autumn". Song Dae-Jin clearly wrote in "The Mid-Autumn of August 15th, the third and a half of August, so China-Autumn." It means that the full autumn of July, August, September and August 15 is just in the middle of the three autumns, so it's called Mid-Autumn. In other words, Mid-Autumn refers to August, the entire agricultural calendar, and Mid-Autumn refers specifically to August 15, the day in mid-Autumn.

It is worth noting that ancient "middle" and "inter" can be used in many contexts. In Qin, "mid-autumn" often refers to the August season of the agricultural calendar, not to the special holiday. This means that the Mid-Autumn concept at the time was mainly a time-related concept, closely related to the season and farming.

The small differences between Mid-Autumn and Mid-Autumn, and the cultural implications.

Strictly speaking, Mid-Autumn refers to the entire August agricultural calendar, while Mid-Autumn refers to the day of August 15. But since August 15 is the middle of the autumn and the middle of the third autumn, they are often mixed in their actual use. This is why they call the Mid-Autumn Festival the Mid-Autumn Festival. This method of naming not only accurately reflects the location of time, but also the philosophies of the ancients with regard to "middle" and "dash" philosophy. The moon is the roundest on this day, a symbol of family unity and good life, and a voice in the middle and balance of the word.

In addition, mid-Autumn is closely related to agricultural production. The August season is the season of ripe rice and fruitful harvests, and the ancients have the tradition of "Autumn News" to thank the Land God for a year of harvest. The Mid-Autumn Festival coincides with the tradition of the Autumn Land, which at the same time provides for the twin meaning of worship and thanksness to farming.
