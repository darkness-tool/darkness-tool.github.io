Why would a geese go south in the fall? It's the greatest migration mystery in nature!

Author: Keung Date of issue: 5 September 2026 Update: 5 September 2026 Reading time: About 7 minutes

As the seasons of fall wind and fall leaves begin, there is always a beautiful scene in the sky. A geese flying high in the sky, in a single human or one-word formation, making a loud sound and flying towards the warm south. This scene, which has inspired the imagination of hundreds of poets since ancient times, has also filled modern generations with wonders: why do geese have to fly to the south in the autumn?

To understand why geese fly south, they must first understand how they live. The geese belong to the typical migratory birds, who have established breeding grounds and winters on both the north and the south of the planet. Every spring and summer, vast lands in the north, such as the Siberian belt, have warm and long weather, with rich waterweeds and insects, are excellent breeding and breeding grounds. So the geese would choose to spend their reproductive years in the north.

The survival of climate change and food shortages

However, the good news is that, with the global and seasonal changes, the autumn is just as fast, and with it is the temperature of a sudden drop. The winter in the north is long and cold, and the ice and snow will cover the whole land, which is already rich in water and grass, and where insects and small animals hide or die. For geese, the harsh environment in the north not only makes it difficult for them to cope with the cold, but also faces a serious food shortage crisis. To survive, migration becomes their only option.

In contrast, the southern part of our country has remained relatively warm during the winter, with rivers and lakes not completely frozen and still providing sufficient sources of food, such as green grass, grain and small aquatic organisms. As a result, every fall the geese travel south, and the essence of this is the life-saving instinct that the living creatures have evolved to adapt to their natural environment and to avoid severe cold and hunger.

It's a brilliant human-style flight code.

In addition to "Why fly," another interesting question is: Why do geese always have to be "man" or "one"? Scientific research has shown that this formation has a high degree of physics and team intelligence behind it. When the head geese fly with wings, the tip of the wings creates an upward flow and spin. If the geese are in the right place, they will be able to gain an extra boost from this rising current, which will significantly reduce the amount of energy they need to travel alone.

Based on the experiments and calculations of aerodynamics, a grouping and grouping of specific groups of geese can consume more than 30 per cent less energy than a single flight. This is the key to ensuring a successful arrival at the destination for the thousands of kilometres of migratory birds that need to fly to the avalanche. In addition, the most experienced geese usually take over as "team captains" in the front, and when the geese are tired as they travel long distances, they retreat to the middle of the team, with other geese taking over the lead.

The southern flying of geese is more than just a beautiful journey of nature, which has brought to light the wisdom and solidarity of wild animals in the face of environmental challenges. And when we look at these birds once again in the autumn sky, there is a sense of respect for the wonders of nature and life.
