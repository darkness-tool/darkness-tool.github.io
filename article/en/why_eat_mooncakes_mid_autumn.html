Why do you have to eat moon cakes in mid-Autumn?

Author: Yuki Date of issue: 12 August 2026 Update: 12 August 2026 Reading time: about 5 minutes
HERO FIGURE

Every mid-Autumn, the family must have moon cakes, but do you really know why they have to? On the night of August 15th, when the full moon was empty, the Chinese world almost had the same image: a family sitting around a table, cutting out sweet mooncakes and sharing the round pastry with the moon. The mooncakes are not just a food, but a symbol of mid-autumn. However, this seemingly natural custom lies behind it a thousand years of history, folk legends and deep cultural connotations. From the rituals of the ancient emperors' ceremonial months, to the pious letters hidden in the cakes of the end of the reigns, to the bridge of the modern people's bond of affection, the story of the moon cakes is much better than you think. Let's start today. Why do we have to eat mooncakes for Mid-Autumn?

To understand the reasons for eating the moon cake, it is necessary to return to the origins of Mid-Autumn itself. Since ancient times, China has been governed by the rituals of spring and autumn. The Law of Honour clearly states that "The Heavenly Sun, Autumn Moon" means that the Emperor worships the sun in the spring and the moon at night in the autumn." Most of the first festivals were scheduled for autumn, but there was not necessarily a full month on autumn day, and then they were fixed on August 15, because the moon was the brightest and the most responsive to the idea of a “Rally of the Moon”. When the festival takes place, people prepare all kinds of round offerings that are full and productive, and it is from them that the moon cakes evolve.

The moon cakes are the first offerings of the festival moon.

In ancient times, the moonbreads were not a simple snack, but an important offering in the service of the Moon. The people believe that the moon is in charge of the harvest and the blessing, and that it may express piousness to nature through its offerings, and pray for a year to come in which the rain will be smooth and the grain will grow. At the end of the service, the family divides the supplies into food, which is a form of sharing and linking between people and God, people and people. This habit of "prestige and eat" has led to a gradual shift of the moon cake from a sacred offering to a holiday meal. At Tang Dynasty, there's a mid-Autumn moon and an apparition of waffles. Tang Goo-Zu Li-yun, who is known as General Lee Jing, gave a round-shaped burrito congratulations to his merchants, who said to the heavens and the moon tomorrow, "The burrito should be invited" and gave it to his fellow citizens, beginning to spread in the middle of the autumn. In other words, it was mentioned that Tang Zhong had been given a gift to the New Surgeon in mid-Autumn with a sarcophagus, indicating that similar customs existed in the court.

In the Song era, these are more popular among the people, and Susu left behind a phrase, "Twisted like a moon, with a soothsay" , which depicts the sweet taste of the mooncake. The name "lunar cake" appeared in Southern Song's Dream Book, but at the time it was just one of the city well snacks that had yet to be fully bound to Mid-Autumn. What really keeps mooncakes and mid-Autumn festivals inextricably linked and gives the meaning of "reunification" is after tomorrow. The Syndicate of August 15th, the month of the Mid-Autumn, the people took the moon cakes and took them for a purpose of reconciliation. It also wrote that the Mid-Autumn Festival was marked by the ‘His festivities’, which were officially called ‘the conglomerates’. After the month of sacrifice, people eat the moon cakes, and each of them is a sign of a family, and even if someone is far from home, a share of them will be reserved for them, and they will express their thoughts and blessings.

The most dramatic legend: the Enormous Letter in the Mooncake.

In addition to official historical changes, one of the most widespread and dramatic expressions of public opinion has to do with the early years of the coup d'état. Zhu Yuanjin and Zhu Bo Wen were planning to overthrow the Won Dynasty, and it was difficult to pass the information because of the fact that the soldiers and soldiers were searching for it. Liu Bo Win came up with a plan to spread the rumour that "the plague is imminent, and only the eating of the moon cakes will save us from disaster" and also hid in the moon cakes a note written "On the 15th Night of August" and sent the message to all the places by means of the moon cakes. By mid-autumn night, the moon cakes were cut open and the letters were seen, and the event began and finally the establishment of the Ming Dynasty was successfully established. After Zhu Won-hyun came to power, he ordered that he be given a moon cake in the Mid-Autumn to remember this history, and the custom of eating moon cakes in the Mid-Autumn has since become stronger. Although the story cannot be directly recorded in official history books and is part of a popular legend, it is a vivid illustration of how mooncakes are a symbol of unity and resistance from food, and explains why younger generations attach special importance to eating mid-twilight.

Circle and sweetness: the most direct cultural password

Why moon cakes, not other food? The answer is in its appearance. In Chinese culture, the circle represents a full, peaceful and united society. The moon is the roundest of mid-fall and fall nights, and people want to be as full as it is, so make the pastry round and call it straight to the moon. A sense of ceremonial solidarity has been strengthened by the fact that the ceremonial bread is usually divided between the elders and the number of the family members, one for each and the absent. As for sweetness, it carries another level of good hope. In ancient farming societies, sweetness is a precious taste for sweet life and happiness. The moonbreads are mainly sweet, and it is the ancients who love and applaud the life of "round and sweet." Even in modern times, the taste of the moon cake has changed from the traditional lotus yolk, five beers, to ice skin, drifting heart, low sugar version, to the core of the circle and sweet.

The moon cakes also carry the meaning of harvest and gratitude. In mid-Autumn, which is the season of maturity for agricultural crops, the ancients staged "Autumn News" to thank the God of the Land for his blessing and pray that the harvest will continue in the coming year. The monthly bread is a tangible expression of this sense of gratitude as a sacrifice and a share. At the same time, it is a medium of expression between relatives and friends. After dawn, the moon cakes became a regular custom, and until today, the Mid-Autumn gift was dominated by the moon cakes, a box of beautiful moon cakes that brought warmth and blessings, whether it was business or a greeting from relatives.

Why do modern people eat mooncakes?

In the 21st century, the way of life changed, and a lot of people began to wonder: "Is the moon cake too sweet to eat?" I'm sorry. It is true that traditional monthly bread is higher than oil, but that does not mean that it is completely abandoned. Appropriate goods, a healthier version, or a process of sharing it with family members are of emotional value. For many people, eating mooncake is not just for the desire of the mouth, but a cultural memory and emotional ritual. When you cut out the moon cakes, smell the familiar scent, talk to your family and enjoy the moon, it's a beautiful vision that has lasted hundreds of years. Even when you're away from home and you get a box of moon cakes from home, you can suddenly feel the warmth of memory. That's the real reason for the moon cake -- it's carrying more than a piece of cake itself.

From the Zhou Dynasty Ceremony to the court of Tang Dynasty, Song Dynasty's poetic depiction, the symbol of Ming Dynasty's unity, to the legends of enlightenment that flowed among the people, the moon bread step by step from sacred offerings to the heart of the celebration. It has a round image of the full moon and family, a sweet taste of a happy life, and a shared process that strengthens the link between people. Whether you like traditional lurking or new tastes or eat moon cakes in mid-autumn, you remind us that in busy life, remember to stop, enjoy the roundest moon in the sky with the people you love, and express in the simplest possible way "I want us to be together forever."

Next time you pick up the moon cake, think about the story behind it. This is not just a piece of cake, but a microcosm of thought, gratitude, unity and hope in Chinese culture. And Mid-Autumn Night, and the full of moon cakes, and the full of sweet and sweet benevolence for you and your family.
