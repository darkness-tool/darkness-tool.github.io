Super-frozen! Why is the hospital's air conditioning always so cold? The truth shocked you!

Do you have that experience? Once you get into the hospital, it feels like you're in the freezer. In the summer, he was sweaty, he came into the hospital hall, he was fluffed with cold air, and he even had to find his coat. A lot of people complain that the hospital is running too low, and that the patient has to wear a thin suit to warm himself. In fact, the air temperature set at the hospital is based on its own expertise and is not downgraded. From infection control, the preservation of medicines, to the need for operating rooms, low-temperature environments are essential for medical safety. Today, the shocking truth behind this frozen air condition will be revealed, so that you will not go to the hospital next time but understand the medical wisdom. It's cold in the hospital, not for the sake of the patient, but for the safety and health of all. Many witnesses — and we, the people who have seen them — have experienced this cold, but the reasons behind them are far more complex than they thought. Not just the hall, the clinic, the room and the operating room, but why? Let's step by step look at this "frozen" hospital air-conditioning secret. It's not just a matter of comfort, it's a strict standard of the medical environment!

First, the most important reason is to inhibit the reproduction and spread of bacteria and viruses. Hospitals are places where patients gather, and the air is filled with microbes, and hot and wet environments allow bacteria to grow rapidly. Low temperatures can significantly reduce bacterial activity and make infectious diseases less contagious, which is the most frequently cited official reason. The importance of low temperatures for the control of infections is more evident, especially during the epidemic. In addition, it removes the moisture from the air, prevents the appearance of the presence and avoids the cross-infection of bacteria on walls or devices. Many medical devices are also afraid of high temperatures, which affect the accuracy of the operation of sophisticated equipment and even cause malfunctions. For example, machines in radiology need to stay at a low temperature to stabilize. These seemingly frozen settings are designed to create cleaner, safer medical spaces. The next time you freeze to shake, think of it as protecting yourself from hidden threats.

Why is the operating room so cold?

The operating room is the air-cooling area of the hospital, and the temperature is usually between 20 and 26 degrees, or even lower. This is because, during the procedure, doctors and nurses wear heavy sterile clothes, mask gloves, and the lights are strong and can sweat and heat. The low temperatures keep the medical team comfortable, sober, and avoid the sweat dripping of contaminated wounds. At the same time, the wound was exposed to air, like a plate of raw meat, and if the temperature is too high, the bacteria tend to breed, increasing the risk of infection. Low temperatures also reduce patient hemorrhage, as cold air constricts the blood vessels and coagulates faster. For patients who are lying on the table, even though they feel cold, the health care will be warmed or used to keep them warm. The same is true of maternity wards, where cold temperatures are conducive to smooth medical operations and inhibit bacteria. These settings are subject to strict regulations, and the Ministry of Health and Welfare has clear standards for air traffic in medical institutions. The cold in the operating room was not intended to cause the patient to suffer, but for the success of the operation and the safety of the patient. Doctors don't tell you on their own, but this freezing environment actually saves thousands of lives. Next time you're in the operating room, remember how many blankets you need, you'll be comfortable.

In addition to infection control, the preservation of medicines and vaccines is also important. Many of the drugs, vaccines and consumables in the hospital need to be stored at low temperatures to avoid high temperatures causing deterioration. The central air conditioning maintains the overall temperature, which prolongs the availability of medicines and ensures the quality of treatment. As soon as some of the drugs leave the fridge, the room is too hot to function fast and the cooling is the best insurance. This not only guarantees the safe use of drugs, but also reduces the waste of medical resources. In addition, health-care workers travel around the world all day, and cold temperatures can reduce their sweat and maintain their concentration. For health care in heavy protective clothing, cooling is the key to maintaining work efficiency. The patient, though cold, is actually more comfortable with fever or fever. It's easier to sleep and it's good for the healing. The cold in the hospital is the result of multiple considerations, not simply saving electricity or ignoring patients' feelings.

What if the patient gets too cold?

As the temperature is too high to affect the overall infection control, the nurses will keep it down. If it's really cold enough, you can ask for extra blankets for the care station, which are usually provided with warm blankets. Some hospitals also offer hot drinks or a whole bed area. But remember, it's necessary to stay a little longer for the sake of health. Especially for post-operative patients, hypothermia reduces pain and pain. Of course, if extreme temperatures were as low as 12 degrees, it could be a cooling malfunction or family disorder, which would have to react quickly.
