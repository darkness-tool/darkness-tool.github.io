The power outage is the truth! Seven reasons you have to know.

Author: Alvin Date of issue: 13 June 2025 Update: 13 June 2025 Reading time: about 6 minutes

After the power outages, what are the unknowable secrets hidden? The power outages can disrupt daily rhythms without warning, regardless of whether the power in the home suddenly goes out or the light in the office is dim. In a short moment, our lives, jobs and safety were affected by power cuts. Have you ever wondered why a power company is trying to maintain a steady supply of electricity every day and still cannot completely stop the power outage? In fact, the causes of power outages vary from natural disasters to human neglect, from ageing of equipment to routine repairs. Understanding these critical factors will help us not only to remain calm in the face of power outages, but also to do it without rain. It is an excellent emergency to get families and company colleagues back to normal life and work in the shortest possible time. Next, let's take down the seven main causes behind the power outage.

Power outages can be broadly classified as "planned outages" and "suspicious outages". The planned power outages are usually announced in advance and are used for network maintenance, upgrades of equipment or routine inspections to influence users. Most of the sudden power outages are due to force majeure or accidents, often accompanied by lack of transparency of information and longer recovery times. The frequency of power outages and the rate of recovery are also quite different in cities, suburbs and remote areas. In some areas, the power supply can be quickly replaced by a storm because of the power grid infrastructure that has been installed; and in remote areas, when poles or cables are damaged, it takes more time to repair the supply of manpower and materials. On the other hand, the user's own use of power loads and electricity safety habits can also be a key variable in the aftermath of power outages. The real power outage is more complicated than we thought.

1. Bad weather

Extreme weather, such as typhoons, heavy rain and hail, is the most common cause of large power outages. The wind tends to cause high-pressure poles to fall or cables to break and cut the circuits directly. A lightning strike may also destroy the transformer or electrical equipment in an instant, causing an instant power loss in the surrounding user. In winter, snow and ice freezes on cable surfaces, increasing the burden of the line and even creating short roads. These natural factors are almost unpreventable and can only be reduced through increased power pole structures and protection. Most power companies conduct road patrols and trim road trees before the hurricane season to reduce the impact of the crashes. The user community can prepare for emergency lighting and mobile power, as well as for the official announcement of power recovery. In this way, when the next extreme weather strikes, it will be easier to face power cuts.

2. Equipment maintenance and routine inspection

In order to sustain the long-term steady operation of the electricity grid, the electric company must regularly arrange for equipment and upgrades. These include transformers, power distribution, wiring, and the strengthening of pole structures. Although most of the inspections will be announced in advance and coordinated for the wrong power outage, some of the emergency repairs may have been delayed on an ad hoc basis, affecting users. The risk of accidental power outages can be reduced by pre-empting the power peak, but there is still a need to pre-empt the power supply and maintain the power system for critical users such as businesses and hospitals. Users can use official websites or APPs to join the announcements, get up-to-date information about the inspections, and prepare adequate water and emergency supplies before power cuts.

Accidents or human factors

Traffic accidents and poor work practices can break underground cables or poles. When large excavators or cranes fail to set the required safety distance at the time of construction, when they touch high-pressure lines, they cause an instant power outage and sparkling. In addition, the construction team may cause short circuits or leaks of electrical protection to jump off, resulting in a wide power outage, without having applied for private power withdrawals, or by digging for work affecting underground pipelines. The incident management also included an on-site screening and repair of damaged equipment by the power company, which could be further extended if communication was poor or the road was in poor condition. As a result, the construction unit and the power company must maintain close contact during the construction of the high-risk area.

Community response and precautions

In the face of the inevitable power outage, communities and families should develop emergency response plans ahead of time. Prepare basic emergency equipment such as mobile power, LED lighting and flashlights to maintain adequate power on mobile phones. Water and food are stored for more than three days and the shelf life is regularly checked. For families with young, elderly or sick children, the necessary medicines and care supplies should be added. If the conditions permit, the installation of residential UPS or small power generators can be considered as backup. The community can also set up mutual support groups to inform each other about the blackouts and share resources. If you want to keep an eye on the power company's official announcement or receive instant updates through the APP, you can quickly determine the recovery and take action in case of power outages.
