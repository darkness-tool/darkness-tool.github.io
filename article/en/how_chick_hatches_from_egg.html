It's amazing how chickens come out of a hard egg. It's not like it's gonna look like it's been exposed all day!

Author: Big Man Date of issue: 22 November 2025 Updated: 22 November 2025 Reading time: about 6 minutes

You can't imagine an seemingly ordinary egg in the middle of a 21-day-long breakout! Every time we look at the look of a crumbed chick, it's so cute, but have you ever wondered how it got out of that hard egg? Many people think that chicks come out just banging on the shell, but the whole process of hatching is like a fine life movie, and it's begun to change incrediblely from the moment of fertilization. At 37.5 to 38°C hens, the embryos in the eggs undergo alarming changes every day. In 21 days, chicks will have to make a change from a small cell to a full rooster, and they'll have to "seek" their eggs to escape! Let's take a look at how the chickens are doing one step at a time. This will be the most powerful hatching record you've ever seen! From day one to day 21, there are different surprises every day. Let's open up the cosmological secrets in the eggs! Promise you won't be able to eat the eggs again with your usual heart!

Day 1-3: From a little red dot to Little Spider

The fertilized eggs have begun to split in the tubes, and within 24 hours of entering the incubator, there will be an "embrace". It's the first piece of chicken to form! By the third day, the embryo had grown eyes, heads, spines and even small wings and leg buds. The embryos looked like a transparent little spider floating on the yolk, holding the nutrients in a vein net. The membrane has also begun to form, protecting embryos from vibrations, while the urine bladders handle waste and breathing. The last three days are the fastest-growing stages, and the number of cells is increasing in geometrical numbers. Many of the incubation failures were caused by a period of unstable temperature or an inappropriate fall. You think the eggs are empty? It's been a hell of a first day!

Day 10: Eggtooth and lethal weapons

By about the 10th day, chickens' beaks will have a hard horn bulge — this is the legendary egg teeth! Egg teeth are not real teeth, but a temporary tool designed to break the shell, which will fall naturally a few days after birth. The chicks have plums, blades, claws and eyelids. It began to extract more nutrients from the yolk, and its body was growing rapidly, and the space in the eggs was shrinking. The urine cyst has covered the whole egg shell and is responsible for transporting oxygen from the gas chamber to the chicken embryo. The chicks' necks are also starting to work on twisting and preparing for subsequent shell breakup. At this point, if you light the eggs, you'll find the eggs almost completely black, except for the air chambers. This means that chicks are ready to enter the final sprint phase!

Day 19-21: Finally, "The Triple Fragmented Shell"

On the 19th day, chicks put their beaks in the gas chamber for the first time in their lungs! You'll hear the sound of "poo-poo" in the eggs. On the 20th day, the chicks began to peck the inside, poking a small hole in the inner membrane with their egg teeth, and at the same time turning the body. This stage is called "the inner hull" and the first crack will appear on the husk, called the "star fissure". Next is the most wonderful "outside pecking," with chickens hitting the same place with their egg teeth, with their shoulders on top. It spins in time, pecks and pushs, and slowly turns the eggshell into a crack. One last push, the eggshell caps are lifted, the chicks pop out like rockets! The whole process could take 6-12 hours. The cuckoo chickens are damp, closed to their eyes and unable to stand, but in a matter of hours they will turn into loose little hairballs. Egg teeth usually fall three to seven days after birth and never grow again. The breakout was done by the chicks themselves, who were only responsible for the temperature and would never help to peck. Do you think chicks are really good after reading it?
