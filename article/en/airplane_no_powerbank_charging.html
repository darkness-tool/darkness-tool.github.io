Surprise! It's dangerous to charge power with mobile power on a plane! Specialists expose hidden risks. Yes.

By Jade. Date of issue: 28 December 2025 Update: 28 December 2025 Reading time: about 5 minutes

Did you know that the power charge on the plane could cause a serious flight emergency? Many passengers are used to charging mobile power when their cell phones are in short supply at a height of 10,000 feet. Recently, however, a number of flights around the world have been frequented by overheated operational power sources, causing major airlines to alert and tighten controls since 2025. It turns out that the platinum batteries in the mobile power source hide a huge risk in the flight environment, which could cause a fire or even threaten the safety of the entire machine if the heat goes out of control. Several airlines, such as Taiwan Chief Rong, and China Airlines, have established a clear ban on the use of mobile power power throughout their flights. Not only should mobile phones not be charged, but even for mobile power itself. What is the reason behind this ban? Why does a seemingly convenient power source become a flight hazard? This paper will shed light on the dangerous regime of the cyanide battery and update the aviation regulations. Passengers must know before going out, so as not to inadvertently harm themselves and others. Let's see what the experts say and how we can safely charge it over long distances. It's absolutely the knowledge that every frequent passenger can't miss!

First, at the heart of the mobile power source is a platinum battery, which has high energy density but is also prone to "hot out of control" due to overheating, short circuits or damage. Hot out of control means that the inside of the battery is rising at a rapid rate, producing a chain reaction, releasing flammable gas and setting fire, or even explosion. In such a closed space as the aircraft's cabin, the consequences of the fire are impossible to imagine. Moreover, the burning of the electric batteries was difficult to extinguish with a general fire extinguisher and required special treatment. Air pressure changes in the flight, and turbulence can add to the instability of the battery. The risk is higher if mobile power is squeezed or collided. There have been many recent incidents in Korea, Japan, etc., in which mobile power has been overheated and self-fired under a luggage shelf or seat. These accidents have prompted the International Aviation Authority and airlines to urgently amend their regulations. Most flights are now strictly prohibited from using mobile power for any charge.

Why is it so dangerous to fly?

When planes fly at high altitude, the cabin is under pressure, but the environment is still different from the ground, with low temperatures and low humidity, which tends to spread heat to batteries. When mobile power is used, the battery heats up, and if it keeps charging, the heat accumulates faster. Affordable or aging mobile power is not well protected and is more likely to be overheated. In addition, if placed on the top shelf, it is difficult for the passengers to find out in time of heat and delays in processing. This is why many airlines require mobile power to be placed in front of their seats or in their pockets. If you can't put your luggage up, you can't see it now. In the past, there have been flights that have been subject to fire from mobile power, resulting in emergency landings or delays. The expert notes that, although the incidence of a battery fire on the plane is low, if it occurs, it is difficult to put out the smoke. The prohibition of use is the safest way to prevent it. Passengers should understand that this is for everyone's safety and not for the purpose of making trouble. Next time we fly, remember to fill up electricity early and avoid relying on mobile power.

As of March 2025, Taiwan's main airlines, Long Rong Rong, Zhang Zhang, and Starwoo, have banned all use of mobile power to recharge. Not only can you charge your mobile phone, but you can't charge your mobile power. The power of movement must be carried with it and cannot be shipped or put on a luggage shelf. At the international level, the use of mobile power on Japanese aviation is prohibited, and there are similar strict rules in Korea and Hong Kong. Singapore Airlines, Thailand, etc. are also following the ban. Carrying capacity limits: Up to 20 for 100 wh and up to 2 for 100-160 wh. More than 160 whes are banned. The mobile power source must clearly indicate wh capacity, and no sign may be confiscated. Ports need to be insulated to avoid short circuits. These regulations have been strengthened in the light of recent incidents of fire in order to protect the flight.

What do we do when we charge it?

If we can't use the power, what about the long-distance phone? The expert suggested that all devices should be fully charged before going out, which is the simplest way. Most modern aircraft seats are equipped with USB chargeholes or AC plugs, which can be used directly by plugging wires. The economy also often has USB Type-A holes, ready to charge all kinds of power. The airport terminal also has charging stations, making more use of these facilities. Select low-capacity mode, shut down the unnecessary app, and extend the continuation of mobile phones. Multiple charge wires, or multiple turn-overs, should be used for different outlets. If the old machine is uncharged, download the content of the offline entertainment in advance. Some airlines offer leased mobile power services, but these are decreasing. Anyway, with power management, you can fly. To comply with the regulations, not only to protect oneself but also to respect other passengers. Passenger Reflection and Expert Appeal

This wave of power bans has made it difficult for many passengers to first hear the news, but they understand the reasons. After all, flying is bigger than everything, an accident could be tragic. Experts have called for a proper brand when buying mobile power, with multiple protection schemes. To avoid cheap or unknown sources and reduce risks.
