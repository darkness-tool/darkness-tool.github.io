Small paper with great wisdom: A4, A5, A6 and B5 sizes of fantastic origin and evolution -Step.

Author: Haru Date of issue: 5 September 2026 Update: 5 September 2026 Reading time: About 3 minutes

We do business and study every day, but do you wonder what their sizes are? Whether it's the A4 paper that's used in the printer or the A5 notebook that's carrying with it, these seemingly normal paper sizes, behind their backs, are actually hiding sophisticated mathematical principles and an interesting historical change.

Before the beginning of the twentieth century A.D., the world's countries were divided and divided. Every country, and even every paper factory, has its own standards, which not only causes a waste of paper, but also severely hinders the international exchange of information and the development of the printing industry.

It's an 18th century gold scale.

The first step in the standardisation of the paper is due to the German scientist Teodor Lichtenberg. As early as 1786, he found out that if the width of the paper is two to one, then when the paper is folded, the width of the new paper remains completely unchanged. It's an amazing and practical feature.

In 1962, the German engineer, Walter Postman, systematized the concept and formally set the German national standard, the famous DIN 476 standard. This standard establishes a modern paper system based on both volume and proportion.

The birth of Series A and Series B.

With the promotion and refinement of German standards, the International Standardization Organization has subsequently adopted and developed a global ISO 216 standard. The A series is a rule used to define basic sizes, e.g., the A0 page size is exactly one square metre, and the fractional order of the A1, A2, A3, A4, A5 up to A6.

At the same time, series B was created as a support series to fill the blanks between the sizes of series A. It's like our usual B5 size, between A4 and A5, and it's very good for books, magazines and notes in a particular format, offering a wider choice.

From the A4 file to the A6 portable manual, these seemingly simple sizes are the great result of the human quest for efficiency and standardization. The next time you pick up a piece of paper, you can take a closer look at this intellectual outcome across the century.
