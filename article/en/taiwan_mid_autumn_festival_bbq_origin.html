The real reason the Taiwanese people have to roast for the Mid-Autumn Festival is the failure of the oven!

Author: Daji Date of issue: 12 August 2026 Update: 12 August 2026 Reading time: about 5 minutes
HERO FIGURE

Every mid-Autumn Festival, there must be barbecues on the back of Taiwan Street. On the Mid-Autumn of August 15th, when the moon was big and round, the same picture almost coincided across Taiwan: The family moved out of the oven, the iron net, the charcoal fire, and started roasting pork chips, sausages, chicken wings, clams and maize in the riding building, alleys, community clearings or roofs. The whole street is like a big outdoor party. This is almost invisible in other Chinese regions, but it has become the most profound mid-fall memory in Taiwan. Why do the Taiwanese have to roast at Mid-Autumn? In fact, there is a whole story behind it, from economic crisis to commercial marketing to social change.

First, there is an important idea: mid-Autumn Barbecue is not an ancient tradition of the millennium. The Mid-Autumn Festival itself was born of the ancient worship of the moon and the celebration of the autumn harvest, which was traditionally the master of the moon, of its bread, of its grapefruit and of its ancestors. Taiwan’s mid-Autumn Barbecue culture is a modern phenomenon that has only evolved in nearly four or 50 years and is almost unique to Taiwan. Many people think that this was all because of the ad "A house of baked meat " , but it's actually just the role of the promoter, and the real origin is going back to the earlier years.

1970s: Barbecue is just the rest of the month. Contents

As early as the 1970s, Taiwan's society began to experience a wave of camping and outdoor rest. At the time, there was a high level of youth self-restraint, with many camping in the suburbs on weekends or holidays, while barbecues were often part of the fire. According to the old newspaper, the Economics Journal of 1973 reported on a picnic in Dream Valley on the evening of Mid-Autumn, which included barbecues and campfires. The 1981 Daily News also refers to the opening of camping and roasting at mid-autumn and autumn nights in tourist areas such as Minderland, which is used to increase the festivities. Most of the roasted meat at this time is an accelerator in a viewing area or a playground that has not yet entered the normal way of celebrating in a normal family.

1982 critical turn: New bamboo ovens are not well sold

It really turned mid-autumn meat from "remain" to "regional pop" in 1982. At the time, Taiwan was a major exporter of manufacturing, and the new bamboo area was a major town for the production of grills and grills, mainly for the European-American market. However, during that year, the global economy moved, with a significant reduction in foreign sales orders, and a large number of ovens piled up in warehouses. In order to digest the storage, the producers turned to the national market, using a strategy to sell more cheaply, and sold the ovens and the simple roasting net at reduced prices in the area of new bamboo, twirling and soak. As a result, in mid-Autumn, a large-scale barbecue appeared in the area. At the time, it was clearly written in the Daily News: "The new bamboo area has been enjoying a month-wide barbecue in mid-autumn season, not only the scenery is full of fire and bears, but some of the people who don't come out set up ovens at the door or on the balcony, smoke and the smell of meat." More directly, the report says: "The main ovens are not selling well, the manufacturers are turning into domestic sales, and the new bamboo area is the base of the ovens." This is the beginning of a national epidemic due to economic difficulties.

After 1986: Soy sauce ads pushed up the tide.

While the roasting wind of new bamboo is already present, strong commercial drivers are needed to spread throughout Taiwan. In 1986, in an effort to promote the new products of barbecue, the soy sauce brand, the classic TV commercial, called "a roasted fragrance" with a powerful slogan. Advertising images are sweet, music is brainwashed, and a perfect picture of the fragrance of the neighborhoods. The following year, the kimran sauce was also weak, with a high level of barbecue ads, and two major brands began the ads during the Golden Period. Instead of creating a roasting habit, these ads have stepped on the existing social foundations and have strengthened local activities into national festivals. This, together with the continued operation of large shops in the late 1980s, led to the sale of roasted meat and food on the eve of mid-autumn, making it easier and more friendly. By the early 1990s, the media had even begun to refer to the Mid-Autumn Festival as the "Bourbon Festival".

In addition to business factors, changes in social structures play an important role. The rapid urbanization and industrialization of Taiwan in the 1980s led many people to leave their homes to work in cities, making traditional family reunions more difficult. Mid-Autumn Day as the Day of the Full Moon provides an opportunity to reconnect with each other. This is an event that requires people to prepare, work together, talk together, have a natural high level of social identity, be it family, neighbor or friend, and get close to the oven. This kind of festivities and participation is better suited to the emotional needs of the society at the time than simply sitting and eating moon cakes.

A unique view of Taiwan's mid-Autumn barbecue.

Unlike barbecues elsewhere, the mid-Autumn barbeque in Taiwan has a few bright features. Most of the sites are not in the suburbs, but are in their own homes, on their horses, in the middle of the community or on their roofs, creating a unique image of "baking on the street night." The choice of food items is also very good: lasagna, sausages with garlic, chicken wings, bouquets, clams, twigs, corn, twigs, soybeans, green peppers can be thrown almost anything on the net. Some even baked toast or used the remaining warm and hot moon cakes by the oven. This is a random, festive and casual atmosphere that makes mid-fall and fall nights a common memory of the whole street.

Of course, this custom has changed over time. In recent years, some young people have started to go to meat shops or choose other ways to celebrate, owing to climate heat, preparation and cleaning problems, rising food costs, and air pollution and noise control. But even so, every time the mid-fall night smoke rises again, many still remember the view of a childhood barbecue at the door. This "new tradition" of less than half a century has been written deeply into Taiwan's holiday genes.

In conclusion, why did the Taiwan Mid-Autumn Barbecue? The answer is not a single factor. It began in the 1970s with an out-of-house boom, which turned into an out-of-home promotion following a setback in 1982 in the out-of-cooking of the new bamboo oven, and became a unique custom today, after being promoted by powerful ads and malls of the post-1986 sauce brand, which finally united the need for emotional connection in urbanizing societies. Next time you smell that familiar barbecue in the mid-autumn night, think about this wonderful story, from the storage crisis to the popular party. This is not just about eating a barbecue, it's about the Taiwanese re-enacting the warmth of the lunar circle in a contemporaneous way.
