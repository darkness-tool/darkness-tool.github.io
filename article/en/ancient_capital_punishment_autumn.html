Twilight puzzle! Why did the ancient death penalty have to wait until after autumn?

I'm sorry. Date of issue: 5 September 2026 Update: 5 September 2026 Reading time: About 4 minutes

In many classical plays or historical novels, we often hear "scrambling after autumn" or "pushing off." Why, however, can the ancient death penalty not be carried out in the spring or in the summer, but in the autumn or the winter of all kinds? It is a system in which ancient people have a unique understanding of the universe, nature and life.

To understand the origins of "scrambling after the autumn" must go back to the Confucian classics of the time. As was already noted in the Episode of the Month, the spring and summer seasons are moments of life and life, when it is not appropriate to carry out a crime of death and to rest.

In the light of the day, there's a sense of change between the sky and the season.

At Handy, the Grand Confucian Council went on to introduce the famous "Aster's Perceptions" and the "Nature of Four Time" doctrine. He believes that there are four seasons in heaven and that the king's political actions must be matched with "the celebration of spring and summer and the punishment of autumn and winter".

In the view of the ancients, spring and summer are a symbol of rebirth and nurturing, and if the prisoners are executed at will, it is no less than in opposition to the heavens, which could cause anger and destruction and loss of harvest. On the contrary, the autumn and winter seasons of weed and yellow, and the slaughter of all things. This is the time when the punishment coincides with the way of heaven.

Institutionalization of the law and the precautionary approach

This tradition was written into the law by successive governments. From the beginning of Tang Dynasty, the law explicitly stipulates that, except for prisoners who are guilty of heinous crimes such as treason, big treason, and who must be "deterred" , ordinary death row prisoners must go through a complex trial process and concentrate on the fall or fall of the frost.

This regulation also reflects the idea of "punishment" as a classic ruler. The long months of imprisonment and review given to prisoners can effectively reduce the number of cases of malfeasance caused by the rush to trial, while giving prisoners more time to reflect.

It's not a mistake.

In addition to the theological and legal considerations, there are practical reasons for the practice. In ancient times, when the country was established, the spring and summer seasons were the busiest spring and summer seasons for farmers, and the country did not want to delay agricultural production as a result of trials and executions.

In the autumn and winter, the people gathered to watch the execution in the legal arena, which had a powerful social warning and shock to serve the purpose of deterring crime and maintaining social order.

As mentioned above, "scratch after the autumn" is not a simple and cruel act, but rather a combination of ancient awe of heaven, the country's legal system and the wisdom of agricultural production. In a long history, the system is a reflection of the unique French culture of ancient Chinese society.
