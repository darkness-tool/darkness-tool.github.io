Autumn miracles! Why do leaves become golden and red? There is an amazing survival wisdom behind it!

Author: Treasure Date of issue: 13 August 2026 Update: 13 August 2026 Reading time: About 3 minutes
HERO FIGURE

And when the green of the summer fades, and the autumn wind blows, and the forest is suddenly replaced by gold and fire. How did this natural play happen every year? The trees are not simply "dressed," but rather for a sophisticated nutrient recovery and self-protection operation in the run-up to the cold. Today, using the most detailed scientific angles, we will dismantle the whole process of changing leaves in autumn.

In the spring and summer, the green leaves are fresh because of the chlorophyll. Green is the key colour for light co-operation, which effectively absorbs the blue and red light in the sun and reflects the green light, so the leaves in our eyes are always green. At the same time, it is responsible for converting carbon dioxide and water into sugar and oxygen to provide the energy needed for the whole tree to grow. At this stage, the chlorophyll content is much higher than the other colours and almost completely covers the presence of other colours in the leaves.

However, chlorophyll itself is not stable. It will continue to break down and will need to be supplemented by a continuous new synthesis of trees. As long as the sun is full and the temperature warms, synthetic speed will keep up with decomposition and green will hold. Once in the fall, the day becomes shorter and the night gets colder, the trees receive a clear seasonal signal and begin to reduce or stop the production of new chlorophyll.

Rating recovery: "Big winter cleaning" of trees

The leaves contain a great deal of valuable nutrients, especially nitrogen, which are indispensable resources for the re-emergence of the trees in the spring. In order not to allow these nutrients to run away with white leaves, trees start a process of aging, decomposing chlorophyll from other proteins into transportable small molecules, and returning branches and roots through the skin. This process is called "ration reabsorption". When chlorophyll disappears, other colours that it hides begin to appear.

In fact, there's always some carrots in the leaves, mainly carrots and chlorophyll. They are yellow to orange, helping to absorb different wavelengths of light in light cooperation and protecting chlorophyll from excessive light. As carrots are much more stable than chlorophyll, yellow and orange appear naturally when green color is lost. In the autumn, most of the trees such as silver almonds, twig trees and turquoises show this bright gold and yellow, because carrots are the lead.

Where does red and purple come from? The secret of varnish.

Not all autumn leaves are just "emerging." In the autumn, trees such as eucalyptus, varnish trees, and yellow curds will be added to the new colored nymph. Plumbing is a water soluble pigment, showing red to purple under acidic conditions and possibly blue purple in a neutral or americ environment. It was not originally in the leaves, but the trees were created in the autumn.

The fusion of pheasant is closely related to the accumulation of sugar. When the base of the leaf is becoming detached, the transport routes for nutrients and moisture are blocked, and the glucose generated from the light co-operation of the leaf cannot be fully transported and accumulates in the leaf. These sugars were converted to luminous luminants by a combination of sunlight and lower temperatures. The more sunlight the day and the cooler the night, the higher the production of pheasant, the more fresh the red. As a result, clear autumns, accompanied by light nights, tend to create the most spectacular red leaf view.

Why do you want to synthesize vasectomy? Two scientific theories.

Now that the leaves are falling soon, why do trees have to spend energy to make red color? There are now two main explanations for this. The first is "light protection theory". When chlorophyll breaks down, leaves lose their original shades of light and energy buffers, are vulnerable to excessive sunlight and generate harmful active oxygen. Plumbs absorb blue and green light as an antioxidant, protect the leaves from photooxidation until the nutrient recovery is completed, and give trees more time to transport the nutrients back into their bodies.

The second is a "co-evolution theory" which suggests that red could be a warning signal to deter insects like aphids. Insects will look for leaves suitable for the spawning of eggs in the autumn, and the red may be interpreted by them as "the tree is strong in defence" or "not well nourished" and less. While there is evidence in support of both theories, RMOs are currently endorsed by more studies, especially in the red-leaved regions of North America and East Asia.

The weather conditions also have a profound impact on the color of autumn leaves. The ideal change in the weather is a warm and warm day and a cool and dry night. This is a condition that allows photosynthesis to continue, and sugar to accumulate, while accelerating chlorophyll decomposition and promoting phyll synthesis. On the contrary, autumn leaves are prone to dimness or yellow if they are damp and warm in the autumn, if they are slow to break down and if they are low in their production. In recent years, climate change has led to high temperatures in the autumn, affecting the red leaf view in some regions, and has become a subject of interest for scientists.

When nutrient recovery is nearing completion, the separation of the bearings is fully formed, the connection between the leaves and the branches is cut, and an autumn wind or rain can bring down the leaves. Following the fall of the leaves, the trees went into hibernation and the nutrients stored will be used again in the spring of the next year to grow new leaves. The gold and the red of fire in the fall, in fact, are the wisdom and splendour shown by the trees to survive. The next time you walk on a forest-filled path, you can take a closer look at this quiet, but beautiful cycle of life.
